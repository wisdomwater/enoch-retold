# Enoch Retold

*A contemplative literary retelling of the Book of Enoch*

## About the Project

**Enoch Retold** is a modern, contemplative retelling of *1 Enoch*, one of the most imaginative works of ancient Jewish apocalyptic literature.

The biblical Enoch is described with remarkable simplicity: **he walked with God**. The *Book of Enoch* expands the mystery surrounding this ancient figure into a vast journey through heavenly realms, mountains of fire, wandering stars, rebellious Watchers, angels, judgment, restoration, and the hidden workings of creation.

This project retells that journey in accessible modern English while preserving the strange and apocalyptic character of the original.

Rather than presenting *1 Enoch* primarily as a historical or theological study, **Walking with Enoch** invites the reader to experience its world through the eyes of Enoch himself.

## Approach

Each reading is written in the first-person voice of Enoch as an old seer recounting what he witnessed.

The retelling seeks to:

* Preserve the essential events, characters, imagery, and theological ideas of *1 Enoch*
* Bring its visions to life through immersive literary description
* Allow angels, heavenly realms, judgment, and other mysteries to remain genuinely strange
* Present the material in approachable, contemporary English
* Divide the larger work into manageable contemplative readings
* Draw spiritual wisdom naturally from Enoch's experiences rather than turning the text into a sermon or commentary

The project is a **literary retelling, not a new translation** of *1 Enoch*.

## The Contemplative Thread

Beneath Enoch's visions runs a question that guides the entire project:

> **How does a person of light walk through a world in which darkness is real without becoming dark themselves?**

Enoch encounters violence, corrupted power, oppression, falsehood, judgment, and civilizations that appear to have surrendered themselves to darkness.

Yet light persists.

The forgotten are remembered. The cries of the oppressed reach heaven. Earthly power proves temporary. What has been wounded can be restored.

The readings therefore return often to themes of mercy, integrity, humility, peacemaking, courage, hope, and resistance to evil without becoming consumed by hatred.

## Structure

The book contains **50 contemplative readings**, each approximately 600–800 words.

Entries follow the format:

```text
## N - Title

[First-person literary retelling from Enoch's perspective...]
```

The readings are designed to be enjoyed individually or sequentially as one continuous journey through the world of *1 Enoch*.

The finished work also includes a **Preface** introducing the motivation and history behind the project and an **Epilogue** sending the reader back into ordinary life with Enoch's invitation to continue walking in the light.

## Historical Context

*1 Enoch* is a collection of ancient Jewish writings composed over several centuries during the Second Temple period. Traditions from the work were known among early Jewish and Christian communities, and the New Testament letter of Jude contains a quotation attributed to Enoch.

Although the complete work disappeared from most Western Christian traditions, it was preserved in Ge'ez through the Ethiopian Christian tradition and remains part of the biblical canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. Aramaic fragments discovered among the Dead Sea Scrolls confirmed the antiquity and circulation of significant portions of the work.

## Purpose

This project is intended for readers interested in:

* The Book of Enoch and other ancient sacred writings
* Early Jewish and Christian spirituality
* Biblical and apocalyptic literature
* Contemplative Christianity
* Literary retellings of ancient texts
* Mysticism and sacred imagination
* The spiritual struggle between light and darkness

Above all, the hope of **Walking with Enoch** is simple:

To enter an ancient and mysterious world, encounter its wonders without explaining them away, and return to our own world a little more determined to pursue goodness for its own sake.

The darkness may grow bold.

**But it does not get the final word.**
