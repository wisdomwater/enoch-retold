## 41 - The House Made New

I saw them brought forward at last, those shepherds who had been entrusted with the flock and had forgotten that the sheep were not their own. Seventy stood bound before the Lord of the sheep, and beside Him was one clothed in white, the scribe who had written all that had been done. Nothing had been lost from that record—not one wound, not one cry, not one life taken beyond what had been permitted.

Before the shepherds were judged, the wandering stars were summoned. They too were found guilty, and I watched as they were cast into a place deep beneath all brightness, where fire rose like pillars from the abyss. Then the seventy shepherds followed them, and the flames swallowed those who had used authority as though it were possession.

As I watched, another chasm opened in the midst of the earth. Fire filled it from wall to wall, and into it were driven the blind sheep who had chosen corruption and violence. Their bones were consumed before my eyes, and I trembled, for judgment is not a thing easily beheld, even when one knows that oppression must someday end.

Do not think, my child, that I rejoiced at the burning. There is a difference between longing for evil to cease and delighting in the destruction of those who have embraced it. The heart can become cruel even while speaking the language of righteousness, and I learned to fear that darkness as much as the darkness I saw before me.

Then my vision turned toward the ancient house. I watched as its pillars were removed, its trees carried away, its ivory stripped from it and taken aside. Everything old was dismantled, not in rage, but with the solemn certainty of something whose appointed season had ended.

And the Lord of the sheep built another house.

It rose upon the same holy ground, but it was greater and higher than the house that had stood before it. Its pillars were new, its ivory brighter and more abundant, and its boundaries opened wide around the sheep who remained. I understood then that God does not merely destroy what has been corrupted; He makes room for what corruption could never build.

All the sheep gathered within it, and then something happened that astonished me more than the falling of kingdoms. The beasts of the earth and the birds of heaven came near, no longer tearing the sheep apart, no longer circling them as prey. They bowed and submitted, and the hostility that had marked the world seemed to loosen its grip upon creation.

The three clothed in white who had guided me through the vision took me by the hand and placed me among the sheep. Their wool was long and pure, white beneath the light, and I saw among them those who had been lost, slain, scattered, and devoured. They were gathered again into the dwelling of the Lord of the sheep.

Then joy entered the house.

The Lord rejoiced because those who had been lost had returned, and because what had been divided had become good again. The sword that had once been given to the sheep was carried back into His house and sealed away. I watched it disappear, and something within me understood that even a righteous sword is not meant to be carried forever.

There comes a day when the weapon must be laid down.

The house became so full that it seemed unable to contain all who entered. Every eye was opened, and all gazed upon the Good One. No veil remained over their sight, no blindness of fear or hatred, and not one among them failed to behold Him.

Then I saw a white cow born among them, mighty, with great horns, and the creatures of the earth trembled before him. Yet the wonder did not end there. The nature of the creatures themselves began to change, until they too became white cattle, as though the old distinctions born of violence and fear were being remade from within.

Another great creature appeared among them, immense and strong, with dark horns upon its head. I cannot tell you every mystery hidden in what I saw. Some things were shown to me as signs whose fullness belongs to God alone.

But I saw the Lord rejoicing over them all.

When the vision ended, I found myself lying where I had begun. I opened my eyes to the ordinary night, yet nothing seemed ordinary anymore. I had seen ages rise and fall, shepherds become destroyers, the innocent scattered, the proud judged, the sword returned, the dead gathered, and the house of God made new.

And I wept.

My tears came so heavily that I could scarcely endure them, because I had been shown the whole long sorrow of humanity as though it had passed before me in a single night. Yet hidden within that sorrow was something stronger than sorrow: a house large enough for the restored, a flock whose eyes were opened, and a Lord who rejoiced when what had been wounded came home.

So I blessed the Lord of righteousness.

For darkness may endure through many generations, and the violent may seem to possess the earth. But no shepherd owns the sheep, no sword is eternal, and no ruin is beyond the power of God to replace with something greater.

I saw the old house taken apart.

And I saw the new one rise.
