## 13 - Where Righteousness Flows Like Water

Through the eastern gates I watched the stars pass, each emerging at its appointed hour and taking the path prepared for it across the heavens. Again and again they appeared, never confusing their courses, never struggling against one another for a place in the sky. And each time I saw them, I blessed the Lord of Spirits, for these great lights were signs written across the darkness, declaring that creation had not been abandoned to chaos.

The longer I watched them, the smaller I felt, yet it was not a smallness that frightened me. There is a humility that crushes the soul, but there is another that releases it from the burden of believing it must hold the world together. Beneath those innumerable lights I understood that the works of God were unfolding long before my birth and would continue long after my bones had returned to the earth.

Then another vision was given to me, a second vision of wisdom. I, Enoch, son of Jared, son of Malaleel, son of Canan, son of Enos, son of Seth, son of Adam, received words that I was commanded to speak to those who dwell upon the earth. I did not receive the whole of wisdom, for what mortal mind could contain it? I received only what my understanding could bear, a portion given according to the pleasure of the Lord of Spirits, yet even that portion seemed to me like a cup dipped into an immeasurable sea.

Therefore hear me from the beginning, and remain with me to the end. Those who came before us spoke what was entrusted to them, and we who come afterward must not close the door through which wisdom enters the world. What is given is not given that we may clutch it to ourselves, but that it may pass through us like water through an open hand.

Three parables were entrusted to me for the inhabitants of the earth, and the first concerned a day when hidden things would no longer remain hidden.

I saw the righteous gathered together, and the light that had lived quietly within them became visible. These were not those whom the world had always called great. Many had walked unnoticed beneath the towers of kings, bearing wounds without becoming cruel, speaking truth without learning hatred, keeping a small flame alive while winds of violence blew around them.

Then their light was revealed.

I saw also those who had built themselves upon oppression, and terror came upon them. Their deeds, which had flourished beneath secrecy, stood exposed before the Lord of Spirits, and there was nowhere left for falsehood to hide. I understood then why judgment is terrible to those who depend upon darkness: judgment is the opening of the shutters.

The mighty had possessed the earth as though the soil itself belonged to them. They had raised themselves above others and imagined their strength would endure because no hand appeared strong enough to overthrow them. Yet before the light upon the faces of the holy, their greatness withered, for there are powers that appear immense only while the world remains dim.

Do not misunderstand what I saw. I did not rejoice because suffering had found new victims. I had seen too much sorrow already to delight in the multiplication of sorrow. But I understood why the oppressed cry out for judgment: there comes a time when mercy toward the wounded requires that the hand striking them be stopped.

The age of the mighty would end. Kings who had believed themselves untouchable would be delivered into the hands of those they had despised, and earthly power would prove as temporary as frost beneath the morning sun. Every throne raised by fear eventually discovers that it has been built upon dust.

Yet these things troubled me, for judgment is not a small mystery. There are words concerning it that even I hesitate to speak, because a man should tremble before declaring the final measure of another soul. I can tell you only what I was shown: there comes an hour when the life we have fashioned becomes visible, when the hidden chambers open, and when what we have loved is revealed by what we have become.

Then the vision changed.

A cloud came upon me and seized me, and the wind lifted me from the surface of the earth. The ground fell away beneath my feet, mountains diminished, rivers became threads of silver, and I was carried upward until I stood near the extremities of heaven. There another world opened before my eyes.

I saw the habitations of the saints.

Their dwelling places were among the holy ones and the angels, and I heard voices rising from them like incense. They were praying, entreating, supplicating before God—not for themselves alone, but for the sons and daughters of men still dwelling upon the earth.

This astonished me.

For after visions of judgment, I expected perhaps to hear accusation. Instead I heard intercession.

Before them righteousness flowed like water, clear and inexhaustible, spreading outward as though a hidden river had broken through the foundations of heaven. Mercy descended like dew upon the earth, quietly touching fields, houses, deserts, cities, the sleeping and the waking, the grateful and those who never knew that mercy had visited them.

I watched it fall.

Dew does not strike the ground with violence. It does not command the grass to receive it or announce its arrival with thunder. It gathers silently upon what is dry, bead after bead, until morning reveals that the whole earth has been touched.

Then I understood something that remained with me through all the terrible visions that followed. Judgment was real; I had seen its books and heard its decrees. Darkness was real also, and there were powers in the world capable of great cruelty. Yet above the earth I discovered that heaven was not filled only with wrath.

It was filled with prayer.

I saw the dwelling of the elect, the habitation of truth, faith, and righteousness, and their number was beyond my counting. The holy ones stood before God like a multitude of lights, yet none seemed diminished by the brightness of another. There was room for them all.

So when you look upon the world and see only the violence of kings, the cruelty of the powerful, or the long shadow cast by human wickedness, remember what I saw above the earth. There are realities that cannot be measured by the noise they make. The storm may shake the doors of your house while dew is already forming silently upon the grass.

You who desire to walk in the light, learn something from that hidden heaven. Do not allow the darkness you oppose to teach you its ways. Pray even while you resist evil; remain gentle even when you must stand firm; seek the ending of oppression without allowing hatred to become your master.

For I saw righteousness flowing like water.

And I saw mercy falling like dew.

And above a wounded world, heaven was still praying.
