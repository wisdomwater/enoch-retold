## 16 - The One Whose Face Was Full of Grace

Then the second parable was opened before me, and its words concerned those who had rejected the dwelling of the holy and denied the name of the Lord of Spirits. I saw a day approaching when all the shelters men had built for themselves would fail them. The earth they had possessed would no longer receive them, and the heavens they had ignored would not open at their command.

But in the midst of this vision of judgment, I was shown something greater than judgment. A throne of glory appeared, and upon it sat the Elect One. Those who had fled for refuge to the holy name looked upon him, and at the sight of him their spirits were strengthened, as though weary travelers had suddenly seen the lamps of home burning through the darkness.

Countless habitations were prepared for them. The Elect One would not remain distant from those who had sought refuge, but would dwell in their midst, and the Lord of Spirits declared that even heaven would be changed for their sake. Its face would be blessed and filled with everlasting light.

Then I heard that the earth also would be changed.

This astonished me, for I had seen much upon the earth that seemed beyond healing. I had seen violence sink into its soil and the blood of the innocent cry from it. Yet the Lord of Spirits did not speak of casting His creation away like a ruined vessel; He spoke of blessing it, changing its face, and causing His chosen ones to dwell upon it in peace.

Those who had made themselves servants of iniquity would not be permitted to trample it forever. Their ways had been seen. The Lord of Spirits had marked their paths, and the peace promised to the righteous required that the reign of those who devoured others should finally come to an end.

While I pondered these things, another vision opened before me.

I beheld the Ancient of Days.

His head was white like wool, and there was something about His presence that made time itself seem young. Before Him, the rise and fall of kingdoms appeared no greater than shadows moving across a field. He was before them all, and when their names had vanished from human memory, He would remain.

But He was not alone.

Beside the Ancient of Days stood another whose appearance was like that of a man. His countenance was full of grace, possessing the brightness of the holy angels without losing the strange familiarity of a human face. I could not turn my eyes from him.

I had seen beings of fire. I had stood before angels whose presence filled me with trembling and had looked upon mysteries that seemed to belong to worlds far beyond the children of Adam. Yet here, beside the Ancient of Days, stood one whose face somehow belonged among us.

“Who is he?” I asked the angel who traveled with me. “From where has he come, and why does he stand beside the Ancient of Days?”

The angel answered, “This is the Son of Man.”

The words remained within me.

“This is the one to whom righteousness belongs. Righteousness dwells with him, and he shall reveal the treasures of what has been concealed. The Lord of Spirits has chosen him, and his portion before the Lord surpasses all in everlasting uprightness.”

I looked again upon his face.

Grace was there.

Remember this, for what I was told next was terrible. The one whose face was full of grace would confront the powers that had crushed the earth. He would raise kings from their couches and cast the mighty from their thrones; he would loosen the bridles by which the powerful controlled others and break the teeth of those who devoured the weak.

The rulers of the earth had forgotten that their kingdoms were given, not possessed. They had lifted themselves upward and refused humility, imagining that riches were strength enough to save them. They trusted the works of their own hands and bowed before the gods they had fashioned, until their wealth became an altar and their own power became the sacrifice offered upon it.

Some even drove the faithful from the places of worship. They rejected the Lord of Spirits and afflicted those who remained true to His name. They believed that because they could silence a human voice, they could silence heaven.

But heaven had heard everything.

I saw their grandeur collapse before the Son of Man. Thrones that had appeared immovable became temporary seats of dust. Those who had made others tremble were themselves filled with confusion, for power built upon fear cannot endure forever before the One whose authority needs no cruelty to sustain it.

This is difficult to behold without allowing anger to awaken within the heart. When we see the violent brought low, something in us may hunger to watch them suffer as they made others suffer. But I tell you what troubled me most about the vision: the face of the one who overthrew them was still full of grace.

He did not need hatred in order to oppose evil.

I have remembered this often.

There is a strength that becomes what it fights. It begins by opposing cruelty and ends by learning cruelty's language. It desires justice, but slowly the suffering of its enemies becomes sweet to it, and darkness has gained another servant while appearing to lose one.

But the Son of Man whom I saw possessed another kind of power. Righteousness belonged to him, and therefore he had no need to borrow the weapons of hatred. His judgment was not the rage of a wounded king defending his pride; it was the setting-right of what powerful hands had bent crooked.

Then from the earth another sound began to rise.

It was the prayer of the holy and the righteous.

With their prayer rose the blood of those who had suffered. Every cry that rulers had ignored, every life crushed beneath violence, every faithful one driven into darkness because they would not bow—their witness ascended into the presence of the Lord of Spirits.

Not one had been forgotten.

So when darkness appears powerful, do not measure its strength only by what your eyes can see. Thrones rise quickly and sometimes endure for generations, while the gentle seem to disappear beneath them. Yet I stood beside heaven and saw how brief the reign of cruelty truly is.

The blood of the righteous rises.

The prayer of the afflicted rises.

And beside the Ancient of Days stands One with the face of a man, whose countenance is full of grace.

I do not understand every mystery surrounding him. I tell you only what I saw.

And when I saw his face, I knew that heaven's answer to darkness would not be darkness.
