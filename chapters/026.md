## 26 - The Oath Beneath All Things

The angel continued to name the teachings that had passed from the rebellious ones into the hands of men. Penemue taught bitterness and sweetness and opened secrets of wisdom that had not been entrusted to humankind in that hour. He taught the making of letters, the laying of words upon a page with ink, so that thoughts might remain after the voice that spoke them had fallen silent.

I wondered at this, for writing appears gentle beside the sword. Ink draws no blood, and a page makes no sound when it receives the words of a man. Yet I was shown that even knowledge may become dangerous when the heart uses it to grasp certainty, bind others beneath its words, or make permanent what should have been held humbly before God.

Men began to place their faith in what their own hands had written. Words meant to bear witness became walls; wisdom became possession, and possession became pride. We had been made to walk in righteousness as the holy ones walk, living before the face of God, yet knowledge severed from purity became another road by which death entered the human heart.

Then Kasyade was named before me. He had revealed darker arts: secret strokes attributed to spirits and demons, injuries against life within the womb, the venom of serpents, and terrors believed to strike beneath the burning noonday sun. I will not linger over those mysteries, for I learned that not every secret becomes holy merely because it is known.

But beyond these forbidden teachings I was shown another secret, older and more terrible than all of them.

It was an oath.

The angel spoke of Beka and of the sacred name revealed among the holy ones, a name before which those who had scattered forbidden mysteries among humanity would tremble. Michael himself had been entrusted with the mystery of this oath. Its power did not lie in syllables uttered as an enchantment, but in the authority of the One whose faithfulness holds creation together.

Then my sight opened, and I perceived what the angel meant.

Before the world had taken its present form, heaven had been suspended by this oath. The earth had been established upon the waters by it, and springs hidden beneath the hills received their paths. I saw waters stirring in the concealed chambers of mountains, seeking the openings appointed for them from the beginning of creation.

The sea also knew this oath.

I beheld it in its fury, rising beneath the winds, waves piling upon waves as though the deep desired to swallow the land. Yet upon the shore lay sand—small grains beneath the feet of men—and the sea came no farther than the boundary appointed to it. I marveled that what appeared weak could stand before what appeared irresistible because the command of the Lord of Spirits rested upon it.

The abyss remained in its place. The sun traveled its road through the heavens, and the moon followed the path appointed to her. Neither wandered from its course, and the stars themselves moved according to their stations; when their names were called, they answered like servants hearing the voice of their master.

Then I heard the winds breathing through the chambers of heaven.

I saw the storehouses of thunder and the terrible brilliance from which lightning breaks forth. There were treasuries of hail and frost, chambers of snow, rain, and dew. What appears to men as weather passing unpredictably across the sky appeared before me as a vast creation held within an order too immense for my understanding.

And all these things praised.

The sea praised by remaining within its boundary. The stars praised by completing their courses. Rain praised by falling, the wind by breathing, the snow by descending in its season, and the dew by gathering silently upon the earth before morning.

None needed to become another thing in order to glorify its Maker.

Then joy moved through the heavenly ones, for another name was revealed among them: the name of the Son of Man.

I saw him seated upon the throne of glory. Judgment was entrusted to him, and before his countenance the works of corruption began to fail. Those who had seduced the earth were bound according to their deeds, and the things they had built through violence and deception began to disappear.

What struck me was not merely that sinners were judged, but that corruption itself was being brought toward an end.

I had walked through visions in which evil seemed almost eternal. I had seen rebellious angels, monstrous powers, kings drunk on dominion, hidden arts, weapons, blood, and knowledge twisted against the purpose for which wisdom was given. At times darkness had seemed woven so deeply into the world that I could not imagine creation without it.

But it was not darkness that held the world together.

That was the secret beneath the secret.

The earth did not rest upon violence. The stars were not sustained by domination. The seas did not keep their boundaries because some greater sea threatened them with destruction.

Beneath everything was the faithfulness of God.

The oath remained. The heavens remained. The stars continued their paths, and the waters knew their appointed places. Even when men abandoned righteousness, the foundations beneath them had not changed.

Then the word of the Son of Man went forth with power, and I saw wickedness receding before his face.

Thus my third parable came to its end.

And when the vision quieted, I understood that those who walk in light need not hold the universe together with their own hands. We are asked only to remain faithful within the small path given to us.

For beneath our feet, beneath the seas, beneath the turning stars, there is already a faithfulness greater than our own.
