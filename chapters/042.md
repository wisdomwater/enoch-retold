## 42 - When Righteousness Awakens

Hear me, my children, for I have seen what becomes of a world when darkness is permitted to ripen. Iniquity does not always arrive with thunder, nor does oppression announce itself as oppression. It grows quietly at first, taking root wherever the strong learn to despise the weak, wherever falsehood becomes useful, wherever the human heart discovers that it can wound another and still call itself righteous.

But there comes a season when what has grown secretly stands openly beneath heaven. I saw sin increase upon the earth—blasphemy, tyranny, impurity, deceit, and every work by which one creature seeks to possess or diminish another. They multiplied until it seemed that darkness had become the natural condition of the world, as though violence had always ruled and always would.

Then the Holy One went forth.

I cannot describe His wrath as you describe the anger of a man. Human anger burns because it has been wounded, and often reaches for another wound with which to answer its own. But the judgment I beheld was like a great fire moving through a diseased forest, terrible in its coming because everything built upon corruption had reached its appointed end.

The towers fell. The strongholds opened. Places from which rulers had imagined themselves untouchable became as dry branches before flame, and the foundations upon which oppression had been raised were uncovered. I saw that evil may build high, but it cannot build deep enough to escape the One who searches the roots.

This is what astonished me: the judgment descended not merely upon the branches of wickedness, but upon its roots. Fraud was torn from the soil that nourished it. Oppression was cut away beneath the surface, where no human sword could reach, and structures that generations had mistaken for permanent vanished beneath heaven.

Yet while I watched these fearful things, I perceived another movement.

Righteousness awakened.

It was as though she had been sleeping beneath the dust of the earth while armies marched above her. Wisdom also arose, not hurriedly and not with the clamor of conquerors, but with the strength of something ancient that had never truly died. What seemed defeated had only been waiting for its appointed hour.

Remember this when the times grieve you. Darkness is loud when it is afraid of ending. Tyrants erect monuments, armies shake the earth, and those who deceive others speak as though their words can alter truth itself; yet the Holy One has appointed days even to these things. Nothing beneath heaven possesses eternity merely because it possesses power.

Therefore I showed my children two paths.

One was the path of oppression. I had seen where it ended, though at its beginning it sometimes appeared broad and easy, paved with advantage and guarded by men whom others feared. Those who walked it learned gradually to grasp, to deceive, to dominate, until they could no longer remember what it was to walk without crushing something beneath their feet.

The other was the path of righteousness.

Do not imagine that this path is always the safer one. There will be times when integrity costs you what deceit might preserve, when gentleness appears weaker than violence, and when truth leaves you standing without the shelter enjoyed by those who speak with divided hearts. Yet walk there still, for the road becomes part of the one who travels upon it.

This is why I warned my children against the double heart. A man may speak of righteousness while secretly loving domination; he may condemn the cruelty of his enemies while allowing their cruelty to teach him how to become cruel. If you stare too long into the hatred of another without guarding the lamp within you, you may begin to carry the very darkness you believed yourself to be resisting.

So arise from slumber.

Let your feet advance in goodness and in mercy. Do not permit the evil of the age to persuade you that goodness is foolish, for I have seen farther than the kingdoms of men, and I tell you that cruelty has an ending. Walk in integrity even when no one praises you for it, and let truth be your companion when falsehood would make the journey easier.

The Great One has appointed a measure to the times. This knowledge does not make suffering small, nor does it ask the oppressed to call their wounds good. It means only that no night has been granted authority to forbid the morning.

I saw darkness pass away.

And beyond it I saw the righteous walking in everlasting light, not because they had conquered the world by becoming more terrible than those who wounded it, but because they had learned another way of walking through it. Mercy remained with them. Integrity remained with them.

And the darkness could not follow where they went.
