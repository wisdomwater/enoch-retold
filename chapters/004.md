## 4 - When the Earth Is Healed

The holy ones stood before the Most High and spoke of all that had been done. They told how Samyaza had taught forbidden arts, how the Watchers had joined themselves to the daughters of men, and how the giants born from those unions had filled the earth with blood. Yet beneath their words I heard something greater than accusation. From the ground itself came the groaning of the dead, rising higher and higher until the grief of earth seemed to gather at the very gates of heaven.

The angels asked the question that every wounded heart eventually asks: *You see all things—why are you silent?* Nothing had escaped the Eternal. He had seen the first descent upon Armon, the secret teachings, the growing violence, the fields emptied by insatiable mouths, and the blood sinking into the soil. Still the holy ones waited, wondering what should be done.

Then the Great and Holy One spoke.

His first word was not destruction, but preservation. A messenger was sent to the son of Lamech, the man who would survive what was coming. He was to conceal himself and prepare, for waters would soon pass over the earth and sweep away the corruption that had overwhelmed it. Though judgment approached like a storm beyond the horizon, a seed of life would be carried through it.

Remember this when you hear of the flood: before the waters rose, God prepared a refuge. Before the old world disappeared beneath the deep, a future was hidden safely within it. Judgment would not have the final word over creation. Somewhere amid the violence, life was already being preserved for a world not yet born.

Then Raphael was summoned.

“Bind Azazyel hand and foot.”

I saw in the command the ending of a terrible freedom. The one who had taught humanity to multiply violence was taken into the wilderness of Dudael, into a place barren and dreadful. Jagged stones were cast over him, darkness enclosed him, and his face was covered so that the light he had rejected could no longer be seen. There he would wait for the great judgment.

But then I heard a command that changed how I understood everything that followed.

“Restore the earth.”

The words descended into me like rain upon thirsty ground. The purpose was not merely to punish those who had corrupted creation, but to heal what their corruption had wounded. The earth that had cried beneath blood was to live again. What had been poisoned by forbidden knowledge, violence, domination, and appetite would not simply be abandoned as ruined.

Gabriel was sent against the giants. These mighty children of the Watchers, who had devoured the labor of men and terrorized creation, would turn their violence upon one another. The very power by which they had dominated the weak would become their undoing. Their fathers would plead for them, longing that their lives might continue for centuries, but the tyranny of the giants would be brought to its end.

Then Michael was sent to Samyaza and those who had descended with him. They would witness the destruction brought forth by their rebellion, and afterward they themselves would be bound beneath the earth through generations, awaiting the day when judgment would be completed. The beings who had crossed the boundary between heaven and earth in order to seize what they desired would find themselves unable even to rise from the depths.

These visions frightened me. I will not pretend otherwise. Fire is terrible, the abyss is terrible, and there are judgments I have seen whose depths I cannot explain to you without trembling.

Yet listen to what followed.

“Let every oppressor perish from the face of the earth. Let every evil work be destroyed.”

Then I saw something green.

From soil that had known blood, a plant of righteousness began to rise. Tender at first, it pushed through the wounded ground, and what had seemed only a vision of ending became a vision of beginning. Righteousness and truth were planted like living things, taking root where violence had once grown.

The earth became fertile again. Trees covered the land—not trees twisted by corruption, but trees of delight, heavy with blessing. Vineyards stretched across the hills, their branches bending beneath fruit until there was more than enough. Seeds yielded beyond expectation, olives filled the presses with oil, and the ground that had once cried because her children were being devoured now gave freely and abundantly.

People lived long in peace. Their children grew without terror. Their days of labor and their days of rest were completed without the shadow of giants moving across the horizon. The earth no longer seemed like a battlefield between appetites, but like a garden in which life had room to flourish.

Only then did I begin to understand what I had been shown.

We often imagine judgment as God's final anger against what he despises. But standing within this vision, I saw another possibility: judgment was also God's refusal to permit the destroyer to destroy forever. The sword was stopped. The devouring mouth was closed. The oppressor's hand was removed from the throat of the weak.

And afterward came the garden.

So when darkness seems ancient and immovable, do not mistake its age for eternity. I have seen mountains that seemed everlasting tremble, and beings stronger than men cast down from their heights. No violence possesses an endless kingdom.

The deepest movement I witnessed was not from life into destruction.

It was from corruption into healing, from blood-soaked earth into fertile soil, from the cries at heaven's gate into vineyards heavy with fruit.

The earth had cried out.

And the One who lives forever had heard.
