## 28 - The Gates of the Sun

After the wonders of the highest heaven, Uriel remained with me, and he turned my sight toward a mystery so familiar that men scarcely think to call it mysterious. He showed me the courses of the lights of heaven: their seasons, their powers, their names, and the appointed places from which they begin their journeys. He told me that this order would continue through the years of the world until the coming of a new work that would not pass away.

Then I saw the gates of heaven.

Six stood toward the east and six toward the west, arranged one beside another with many windows opening around them. Through these gates the sun and moon went forth and returned, while the conductors of the stars kept their appointed courses. I had watched sunrise countless times upon the earth, but never before had I seen what Uriel showed me—the hidden order beneath that ordinary blaze of morning.

Then the great luminary appeared.

The sun came forth like an orb filled completely with splendid and flaming fire. Wind moved its chariot, bearing it upward until its light spread across the face of heaven. I could scarcely look upon it, and yet Uriel regarded its course with the calmness of one who knows the road of a familiar traveler.

He showed me how the sun went down through the western gates and returned by the north toward the east. There it entered again into its appointed gate, and morning was born once more. What men below experienced as dawn and dusk, waking and sleeping, Uriel showed me as a great procession that had continued since the ancient ordering of the world.

At first the sun went forth through the fourth gate. Twelve windows stood there, and at their appointed times flame issued from them. For thirty days the great light traveled this road, rising from the eastern gate and descending through its companion in the west.

And the days began to lengthen.

Uriel taught me to count their portions. The day became ten parts and the night eight. Then the sun moved toward the fifth gate, and daylight stretched farther across the earth until the day became eleven portions and darkness only seven.

Still the sun traveled.

It entered the sixth gate and remained upon that course for its appointed days. Then light reached its fullness: twelve portions belonged to the day and six to the night. I imagined the earth beneath that long brightness—fields ripening beneath the sun, leaves opening toward its warmth, men laboring until shadows stretched across the ground.

Yet even at the height of its strength, the sun did not remain.

Its journey turned.

From the sixth gate it began the long return, and daylight surrendered what it had gained. Eleven portions became ten, and the night slowly reclaimed the hours that light had occupied. The sun passed again through the fifth gate and then the fourth, until day and night stood equal before one another, nine portions against nine.

There was wisdom in that equality, but also wisdom in its passing.

The sun continued toward the third gate. Darkness grew to ten portions while daylight diminished to eight. Through the second gate the night became eleven and the day seven, and at last the sun entered the first gate, where night reached twelve portions and the day only six.

Then the turning began again.

I watched this vast breathing of light and darkness, light expanding across the earth and then yielding, darkness expanding and then yielding in its turn. Neither could grasp the world forever. The brightest season carried within itself the beginning of its shortening, and the longest night already stood upon the threshold of returning dawn.

Uriel did not call this failure.

He called it order.

I had seen kings who could not endure such wisdom. When their strength increased, they desired still more strength. When their territories widened, they pushed their boundaries farther. They could not imagine greatness except as endless increase, and so they grasped until the very things they possessed began to possess them.

But the sun did not grasp.

At the height of summer it surrendered the light it had gained. At the depth of winter it did not despair because its hours had become few. It simply kept the path appointed to it, passing faithfully from gate to gate.

I thought also of the darkness within a human life. There are seasons when light seems abundant and the presence of the Lord feels near enough to touch. There are other seasons when the gate seems to narrow, the days of the soul grow short, and darkness occupies more of the inward sky than we would choose.

Do not too quickly believe that God has abandoned the heavens because night has grown long.

I watched the sun complete its circuit and return again toward its beginning. Nothing had been lost by its yielding. The path remained beneath it, the gates remained before it, and the same faithfulness that carried it into darkness carried it again toward light.

Perhaps this is one reason the heavens were shown to me after visions of judgment and fire. I had seen powers rise and fall, angels rebel, kingdoms perish, and the earth tremble beneath violence. Yet above all of it the great luminary continued quietly through its gates.

Morning. Evening. Lengthening. Shortening. Departure. Return.

The sun did not hurry.

It did not resist its season.

It simply traveled the road given to it.

And as I watched it begin its circuit once more, I wondered whether righteousness might sometimes look like this: not conquering the darkness, nor fleeing from it, but continuing faithfully through the appointed gate, trusting that no night possesses the heavens forever.
