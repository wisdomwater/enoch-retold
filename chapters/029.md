## 29 - The Light Given by Measure

Uriel continued to show me the journey of the sun, for its course had not ended when darkness reached its fullness. From the first gate it began to return, passing again through the roads appointed from the beginning. For thirty days it rose and descended, and the night surrendered one portion of its length, becoming eleven parts while the day grew again to seven.

Then the sun entered the second gate of the east. Another thirty days passed beneath its light, and the darkness diminished again until night was ten portions and day was eight. From there the great luminary moved toward the third gate, rising and setting according to its appointed number of days, until once more the darkness and the light stood equal.

Nine portions belonged to the night.

Nine belonged to the day.

Uriel told me that by these movements the year was completed, three hundred and sixty-four days gathered into a single great circuit. The changing length of daylight and darkness was not without order, nor did the seasons stumble accidentally into one another. The sun itself made the difference by its progress, moving continually from gate to gate according to the command established for it.

I watched it travel and wondered at its constancy.

The sun never appeared weary of rising. It did not slow because it had crossed the heavens yesterday, nor complain that tomorrow would require the same journey. Day and night it continued in its chariot, going out and returning, never abandoning the path given to it.

Men often desire faithfulness to feel glorious. We imagine that if the Lord has appointed us some purpose, every step toward it must burn with meaning. Yet I looked upon the greatest luminary in the heavens and saw that much of its glory consisted simply in returning to the same path again and again.

There is holiness in returning.

The sun rose because it was time to rise. It descended because it was time to descend. It did not demand that every morning be different from the morning before, and still every dawn brought life to the earth.

Then Uriel turned my sight toward another luminary.

I beheld the moon.

Its orb was great, and it too traveled upon a chariot moved secretly by the wind. Yet unlike the sun, the moon did not possess the same fullness of light. Light was given to it by measure.

Those words remained with me: *given by measure.*

I watched the moon begin its course almost entirely clothed in darkness. Its roundness was present even when its brightness was not. To the eye it seemed diminished, but nothing had been taken from what it was; only the portion of light visible upon it had changed.

Then, little by little, it received.

Uriel showed me its portions and their increase, how its light grew according to number and appointed time. What began as the faintest brightness along the darkness expanded night after night. The moon did not create the radiance it carried; it received light and bore that light into the night.

I found this more beautiful than if it had shone by itself.

The moon needed no shame because its light was borrowed. It did not hide because the sun possessed greater brilliance. It received what was given and became luminous with it.

Perhaps we are more like the moon than the sun.

There are days when a person feels almost entirely dark, when only the thinnest line of light seems to remain along the edge of the soul. We look upon others who appear filled with brightness and imagine that something has gone wrong within us. We forget that the moon is still whole when only a crescent can be seen.

I had learned this among visions more frightening than the night. I had fallen upon my face before angels. My strength had left me before the Ancient of Days. Again and again another hand had lifted me, another voice had explained what I could not understand, another light had been given because I possessed none of my own.

I was not ashamed of this.

The creatures closest to God did not teach me that holiness meant possessing everything within oneself. The heavens themselves seemed to proclaim another wisdom: receive what is given, carry it faithfully, and do not despise the measure entrusted to you.

The moon began its month beside the sun, scarcely visible. Then it moved away, bearing into the darkness the portion of brightness it had received. Night by night that brightness would grow, and later it would diminish again.

Yet whether full or hidden, the moon remained upon its path.

So too there will be nights when you seem filled with light, and nights when you can find only a narrow silver edge within yourself. Do not mistake the changing measure of what you can see for the measure of what God has made you.

The moon does not cease to be the moon when darkness covers its face.

And even the smallest portion of borrowed light is enough to shine in the night.
