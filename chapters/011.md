## 11 - The Tree of Unfading Fragrance

I journeyed onward until seven mountains rose before me, splendid beyond the mountains known to men. Three stood toward the east and three toward the south, each rising behind another as though great hands had set them there to strengthen one another. Between them opened deep valleys, separated by walls of stone, and in their midst stood the seventh mountain, greater than all the others, its long summit shaped like the seat of a throne.

Trees surrounded those heights, and their fragrance drifted through the valleys like an invisible river. I had walked among the trees of the earth and had been shown wonders that belonged to Eden, yet among them all I had never known a scent such as the one that came from a tree growing near that central mountain. Its leaves did not wither, its blossoms did not fall, and its bark showed no mark of age; clusters of beautiful fruit hung from its branches, full and heavy like the fruit of the palm.

I drew near and stood beneath it, breathing deeply, for the fragrance seemed to pass beyond my breath and enter some hidden chamber within me. “Look,” I said to the angels beside me. “How beautiful is this tree. Its leaves delight the eye, and its fruit awakens desire merely by being seen.” Yet even as I admired it, I knew that what stood before me belonged to a mystery greater than its beauty.

Michael, the glorious one who presided among the angels accompanying me, turned toward me. There was something almost gentle in his question when he asked why I was so curious concerning the fragrance of this tree. I told him what was true: I desired instruction concerning everything I had been shown, but concerning this tree my desire to understand was especially great.

Then Michael directed my eyes from the tree toward the mountain whose summit resembled a throne. He told me that the great and everlasting King would one day descend in goodness and sit upon that height when He came to visit the earth. The mountain was therefore not merely a mountain, nor was the tree merely a tree; I was standing before things that waited for an appointed hour.

No mortal hand was permitted to seize its fruit. No king could command that it be harvested, no wealthy man could purchase it, and no warrior could climb the mountain and claim it by strength. The tree waited untouched until the great judgment, when violence and oppression would at last meet their end, and afterward its fruit would be given to the righteous and the humble.

This astonished me, for among men it is usually the powerful who reach the pleasant things first. They take the fertile fields, the finest houses, the richest fruit, and leave the weak to gather what remains. But here I saw a tree whose sweetness could not be conquered. It belonged to those who had learned another kind of strength—the strength of humility, of faithfulness, of refusing to become cruel merely because cruelty surrounded them.

Michael told me that life would be planted in the holy place near the habitation of the everlasting King. Those who received the fruit would rejoice before the Holy One, and its sweetness would not merely touch their tongues. Its fragrance would enter their bones, as though life itself were sinking into everything that had once grown weary within them.

There would come days, he said, when sorrow and distress would no longer pursue them. Trouble would not forever have the final word over the children of earth. I had seen prisons prepared for rebellious spirits, burning places, terrible abysses, and regions that made my heart tremble; yet now I understood that the purposes of God were not revealed only in fire and judgment. Somewhere beyond the ending of wickedness stood a tree heavy with fruit.

I blessed the Lord of glory because He had prepared such a thing.

From there I traveled toward the middle of the earth, and the land changed before me. I entered a fertile country where branches continually put forth new growth, and beneath a holy mountain water emerged and flowed eastward before turning toward the south. Other mountains rose nearby, and between them narrow valleys descended into rock, some watered and green, others deep and dry.

One valley troubled me. Blessed land surrounded it, lofty trees stood near it, and waters moved through the country, yet the valley itself seemed marked by another destiny. I asked Uriel why such a place existed in the midst of so much beauty.

He told me that it was appointed for those who raised their voices against the glory of God, those whose speech had become hardened in defiance and whose words poured darkness into the world. There judgment would be revealed before those who had received mercy. Hearing this, I became aware again of the terrible weight carried by the human tongue: a word cannot be seen once it leaves us, yet it may wound, accuse, deceive, kindle hatred, or darken another soul.

Still, the valley did not stand alone.

Beyond it were mountains. Beyond it were living trees. Beyond it waters descended like many cataracts, spilling eastward and westward through the land while dew rested upon the earth. Judgment occupied its valley, but it did not possess the whole creation.

Remember this when you walk through your own dark valleys. I have seen places of terror that seemed without end, yet I continued walking and found water beyond them. I have seen what becomes of violence, pride, and hatred, but I have also stood beneath a tree that cannot wither.

The darkness is real, but it is not the whole landscape.

So I blessed the everlasting King and continued toward the east, listening to the sound of falling waters. The fragrance of that hidden tree seemed still to linger within me, and I carried its promise onward: somewhere in the purposes of God, guarded beyond the reach of grasping hands, life waits for the humble like fruit upon an everlasting branch.
