## 19 - The Sign Above the Waters

In the valley of fire I saw instruments being fashioned. There were fetters of iron unlike any chains made by human smiths, for though they appeared strong enough to bind mighty beings, they possessed no weight that I could understand. They lay waiting in that terrible place, and the sight of them filled me with dread.

I turned to the angel of peace who remained beside me. “For whom are these fetters prepared?” I asked. By then I had learned that the most frightening questions are sometimes best asked in the presence of peace.

“These are prepared for the host of Azazeel,” he told me. The rebellious ones who had corrupted those dwelling upon the earth would themselves be bound and delivered to judgment. Those who had taught violence would discover a power they could not overcome, and those who had spread corruption among humanity would descend into the lowest condemnation.

Then I heard the names of Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, and Phanuel. They would be strengthened in that day and rise against the rebellious host, casting them into blazing fire according to the command of the Lord of Spirits. The powers that had seduced humanity and made themselves ministers of darkness would not be permitted to wander through creation forever.

There was severity in this vision, but I saw also a boundary.

Evil had crossed boundaries from the beginning. It reached for what was not given, violated what should have been protected, and taught others to dominate rather than tend the earth. Now the Lord of Spirits declared that corruption itself would be restrained.

But then the vision widened, and I saw waters.

The storehouses above the heavens opened.

The fountains beneath the earth opened also.

Waters descended from above while waters rose from below, until the world seemed caught between two immeasurable seas. The ancient boundaries broke apart, and the waters mingled as they covered the earth.

I knew then that I was being shown a terrible memory of judgment.

The world that humanity had filled with violence was swallowed by waters it could not command. Those who had imagined themselves masters of the earth discovered how small they were before the forces hidden within creation. The fountains opened, the heavens poured themselves downward, and all the achievements of men disappeared beneath the flood.

Yet what followed astonished me.

I heard the Ancient of Days speak concerning the destruction.

No triumph filled His words.

Instead there was grief.

He declared that never again would He deal in this manner with all who dwell upon the earth. Then He swore by His great name and placed a sign in the heavens, a witness between God and the creatures below for as long as heaven stretched above the earth.

I looked upward.

After so much water, after so much death, after a world disappearing beneath judgment, there was a sign of fidelity in the sky.

I understood then that the heart of God could not be known merely by looking at destruction. Judgment was real—I had seen too much to deny it—but destruction was not the deepest desire of the Lord of Spirits. Above the waters He placed remembrance.

There are times when human beings imagine wrath more easily than mercy. We know how to make enemies and preserve grievances across generations. We know how to strike back when wounded and call our retaliation righteousness. But the Lord of Spirits placed a boundary even upon judgment.

The sign in heaven testified that wrath would not be permitted to become the everlasting order of creation.

Yet neither did the covenant make evil harmless.

I saw the Elect One seated upon the throne of glory, and the kings and mighty ones of the world were summoned to behold him. Azazeel and his hosts would be judged before him, for mercy does not require heaven to pretend that corruption is goodness. What destroys must finally be confronted if what has been wounded is ever to heal.

Then I saw other hosts of angels bound within networks of iron and brass. They moved under confinement toward the deep recesses of the valley, toward those with whom they had joined themselves in rebellion. The alliances formed in darkness had become chains.

After this I saw kingdoms convulsing upon the earth.

Princes conspired against princes. Kings were hurled from their thrones, and warriors sprang upon one another like lions from their dens and famished wolves into a flock. Armies crossed the land, horses thundered over roads and threshing floors, and cities became obstacles in the path of men who desired only conquest.

Then something even more dreadful happened.

Men ceased to recognize one another.

Friend turned upon friend. Brother no longer knew brother. Sons raised themselves against fathers and mothers, until bodies covered the ground and the violence men had directed outward curled back upon itself.

I watched with sorrow.

This is one of the darkest mysteries I was shown: evil eventually devours its own fellowship. Those united only by hatred remain united only while they share an enemy. When the enemy disappears, the hunger remains.

Violence promises strength but cannot create communion.

Hatred can gather a crowd but cannot make a family.

At last I saw the mouth of the abyss open beneath them, swallowing those who had given themselves entirely to destruction. The vision was terrible, and I wished to turn my face away.

But then I remembered the sign above the waters.

Do not forget it when you read of fire, chains, armies, and judgment. I did not see a God indifferent to the wounds of the world, nor did I see a God who called darkness light merely because judgment frightened us. I saw the Lord of Spirits restraining what destroys—and then placing a witness in heaven that destruction itself would not have the final word.

Perhaps this is one of the hardest ways to walk in the light.

To oppose evil without becoming possessed by the desire to destroy.

To seek justice without feeding upon vengeance.

To remember the wounded without forgetting that hatred can wound the one who carries it.

I saw what became of beings who gave themselves wholly to domination. Their strength became their chains, their alliances became prisons, and their violence eventually turned inward.

But above the waters I saw another way.

A sign stretched across heaven.

After judgment came covenant.

After destruction came remembrance.

And over a world that had drowned beneath its own violence, the Lord of Spirits placed a promise that even wrath would have a boundary.
