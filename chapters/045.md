## 45 - What Heaven Remembers

I saw the prayers of the righteous rise.

Many had spoken them in places where no earthly ear cared to listen. Some had whispered from beneath the hand of oppression; some had cried aloud and been mocked; others had carried their prayer silently because grief had taken language from them. Yet not one vanished into emptiness, for I saw their prayers ascend before the Lord, where the suffering forgotten on earth was remembered in heaven.

Then I saw those who had caused that suffering brought into judgment. Every circumstance of their deeds was made known before the Great and Holy One, and shame covered faces that once had been lifted proudly over others. They had believed power could decide what would be remembered, but heaven kept another record.

I saw the wealthy boasting among themselves, saying, “We have gathered everything our hearts desire. Our silver is abundant, our barns overflow, and nothing remains beyond our reach.” Their possessions had become permission in their own eyes, so that because they *could* do whatever pleased them, they imagined they had the right to do it.

They clothed themselves magnificently. Gold flashed upon them in the sunlight, purple garments moved about their bodies, and honor surrounded them wherever they went. Men lowered their heads when they approached, and those who saw only the outward appearance might easily have mistaken prosperity for the favor of heaven.

But I watched their glory run through their fingers like water.

No man closes his fist tightly enough to hold a river. Wealth departed, honor faded, garments returned to dust, and the authority that had once caused others to tremble became a memory. What they had accumulated through injustice could not purchase even one moment when the appointed hour arrived.

Yet I was shown something deeper than the passing of riches. The evil that filled the earth had not been forced upon humankind by mountain or valley, nor had the soil whispered oppression into our hearts. Men themselves had devised ways of harming one another and then blamed the world, necessity, fate, or even heaven for what their own hands had chosen.

This troubled me greatly.

For it is easier to imagine darkness as something entirely outside us. We would rather believe that wickedness descended upon us like a storm, leaving us innocent beneath its rain. But I had seen enough of heaven and earth to know that the human heart can become a workshop where cruelty is fashioned, polished, justified, and finally called wisdom.

Therefore I swore by the Holy and Great One that no oppressive act remains secret forever.

I saw the record in heaven.

Day after day the deeds of humankind were written there. The blow delivered where no witness stood, the silver taken unjustly, the lie recorded as truth, the suffering enjoyed because it belonged to an enemy—all appeared before the Most High. Earth may conceal a deed beneath stone, silence, fear, or the passing of generations, but heaven does not become forgetful merely because men do.

This should cause the oppressor to tremble.

But, my children, it may also give courage to those whose cries seem unanswered. When you suffer and no one believes you, you are not therefore unseen. When truth is buried beneath the testimony of the powerful, truth itself has not died.

I spoke woe against those who rejoiced in the suffering of the righteous. I spoke against those hardened in heart, who fed themselves upon the abundance God had placed upon the earth while allowing others to bleed beneath them. How strange that a man should receive bread from the generosity of the Most High and use the strength that bread gives him to strike another creature fashioned by the same hand.

I spoke also against those who wrote falsehood and preserved wickedness in words. A lie written carefully is still a lie. Ink cannot make falsehood true, nor can repetition turn injustice into righteousness.

Yet as I pronounced these woes, I remembered my tears.

Judgment is terrible precisely because the lives before the Judge were made for something better. I did not behold sinners as creatures created for destruction, but as human beings who had so repeatedly chosen oppression that they had bound themselves to what destroys. This is why I warned my children so urgently: do not wait until cruelty feels natural before turning away from it.

Listen while your heart can still hear.

Do not rejoice when another falls. Do not let hatred persuade you that another person's suffering is sweet. Do not imitate the oppressor in the name of defeating oppression, lest you overthrow his throne only to discover that you have seated his spirit within yourself.

Walk instead beneath the gaze of the One who remembers.

Let your hidden deeds be deeds you need not fear to see uncovered. Let the record of heaven contain mercies no one noticed, truth spoken when lying would have profited you, bread shared without witnesses, and wounds you refused to return for wounds received.

For everything hidden shall someday stand in the light.

And what earth forgets, heaven remembers.
