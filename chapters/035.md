## 35 - When the Earth Cried Out

I lifted my hands toward heaven and prayed as my grandfather had taught me. They were only human hands, and the voice with which I called upon God came through a mouth of flesh, carried upon breath that had been given to me. Yet this humbled me rather than silenced me, for the One who formed the tongue of mortal man must also have intended that we use it to speak—to one another in tenderness and truth, and to him when words are almost too small for what the heart must say.

“Blessed are you, O Lord, great and mighty King. The heavens are your throne and the earth your footstool. Nothing escapes your sight, nothing exceeds your power, and nothing hidden beneath the darkness is concealed from you.”

Then I pleaded for the world.

I had seen the rebellion of heavenly beings and the corruption spreading among mortal flesh. I knew judgment was approaching, yet I could not ask for annihilation. “Leave a seed upon the earth,” I prayed. “Do not let the human race disappear. Let something righteous take root again, that the earth may not remain empty forever.”

I asked God not to hide his face from me.

Years before these things unfolded, when I was still young and had not yet married your mother Edna, another dream came to me. I tell it to you now, Mathusala, because what appeared in that vision was the story of generations concealed beneath the shapes of beasts. I did not understand every mystery when I first saw it, but I knew that I was looking upon something ancient and terrible.

From the earth there arose a great white bull.

After it came a heifer, and then two others appeared, one black and one red. The black struck the red and pursued him across the earth until the red one vanished from my sight. The heifer searched for him, wandering and lamenting, but she could not find him.

Her grief filled the vision.

She searched the places where he had been and found only absence. Her cries seemed to pass over the empty earth until the first white bull came near her, and then at last her lamentation ceased. Afterward she brought forth another white bull, and from these came many more—cattle spreading over the earth, generation following generation.

I watched another white bull grow until he became mighty, and many white cattle followed after him. Life multiplied. What had begun with blood and grief did not end there; children were born, families increased, and the earth filled with living creatures.

But then I lifted my eyes toward heaven.

A star fell.

It descended from the heights and came among the cattle, feeding in their midst as though it belonged upon the earth. Soon I saw other stars leaving heaven, one after another, descending toward the place where the first had gone.

I had already been shown the stars in their appointed stations. I knew the beauty of a light that remains where God has placed it. Therefore the sight of stars abandoning heaven filled me with dread before I understood what they intended.

They came among the young cattle.

Yet their manner changed, and they behaved like horses driven by a strange desire. They approached the young females, and from those unions came creatures that should never have been born—elephants, camels, and asses, immense and unnatural among the herds. The cattle recoiled from them in terror.

Then violence erupted.

The creatures bit with their teeth and struck with their horns. They swallowed and devoured the cattle around them, turning pasture into slaughter. The young cried out, herds scattered in panic, and the children of the earth fled before beings whose strength they could not withstand.

I watched them strike and consume one another until the earth itself cried out.

I do not know how to describe that sound to you. It was deeper than the voice of any creature, as though the soil remembered every drop of blood spilled upon it and could bear the burden no longer. The ground beneath all living things had become a witness against the violence committed upon its face.

Then I remembered my prayer.

*Leave a seed.*

For when darkness grows powerful, the temptation is to believe that violence has become the deepest law of creation. The strong devour the weak, fear breeds more fear, and those who are wounded learn to wound in return. Soon every creature seems to possess teeth and horns, and each generation inherits the terror of the one before it.

But I had seen the heavens.

I knew violence was not their first ordinance.

The sun does not conquer the morning; it receives its appointed hour. The moon does not seize its light; it is given light and releases it again. The stars were beautiful while they remained within their courses. Disorder began when creatures abandoned their place and reached for what had not been entrusted to them.

So I raised my eyes again.

Above the terror, heaven was still there.

And as the earth cried beneath me, I saw forms emerging from the heights—the likeness of white men descending from heaven. One came first, bright against the darkness, and others were behind him.

I did not yet know what they would do.

But I knew what their coming meant.

The cry of the earth had been heard.
