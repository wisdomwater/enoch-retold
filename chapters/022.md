## 22 - The Cords of the Righteous

I watched the angels disappear toward the north, their long cords trailing behind them, and I wondered what boundary of creation they had been sent to measure. The angel beside me saw the question upon my face and said, “These are the measures of the righteous.” Then I understood that the cords were not given for the dividing of kingdoms or the marking of possessions, as men stretch ropes across the earth and say, *This belongs to me.*

These measures belonged to another kind of inheritance. They were given to those who lean upon the name of the Lord of Spirits, and by them the words of righteousness would be established. What men had hidden beneath the earth would be uncovered, and what had vanished beyond remembrance would be brought again into the light.

Then I was shown something that caused my heart to tremble with wonder. Those who had perished in forgotten places were not forgotten: those whose bones had whitened beneath the desert sun, those whose bodies had disappeared beneath the waves, those who had been torn by beasts until no trace of them remained. The Lord of Spirits had lost none of them.

No wilderness was distant enough to hide them from him. No sea was deep enough to keep what it had swallowed. I heard it declared before heaven that none could perish in the presence of the Lord of Spirits, and I began to understand that there is a difference between disappearing from the earth and disappearing from God.

Then a command passed through the heights, and all the powers above received it together. A splendor like fire moved among them, and their voices rose until heaven itself seemed to become a living song. They blessed the Lord of Spirits with wisdom and with the breath of life, and the sound was so great that I felt it not only around me but within me, as though my bones had become strings trembling beneath the praise of heaven.

And I saw the Lord of Spirits upon the throne of his glory.

Before him the Elect One was seated, the one who had been hidden and preserved for the appointed hour. A balance was placed before him, for he would weigh the deeds of the holy and uncover their secret paths. Nothing concealed within a creature could remain concealed before that countenance; yet I did not see the frantic judgment of earthly rulers, who condemn quickly because they see so little.

Around the throne gathered beings whose appearance I scarcely possess words to describe. Cherubim and Seraphim were there, and the Ophanin, and angels clothed in power, rank beyond rank, brightness beyond brightness. Yet all their differences vanished into one voice as they praised the Lord with the spirit of faith, wisdom, patience, mercy, judgment, peace, and benevolence.

I marveled that mercy and judgment were spoken in the same breath.

Among men, we often separate what heaven holds together. We call anger justice and imagine cruelty to be strength; or we call indifference mercy because we are afraid to confront what destroys the weak. But before the throne I heard righteousness praised beside patience, judgment beside peace, power beside benevolence, and I knew that the holiness of God cannot be reduced to any single passion of the human heart.

Then the command of the Lord of Spirits went forth toward the rulers of the earth: “Open your eyes.”

I saw kings and princes, the exalted and those who possessed lands as though the soil itself belonged to them. They lifted their faces and beheld the Elect One seated upon the throne, clothed with the spirit of righteousness. The word of his mouth went forth against wickedness, and suddenly the greatness of those rulers appeared very small.

Their fortresses could not shelter them. Their armies could not stand between them and truth. Trouble seized them like the pains of a woman laboring to give birth, and they looked upon one another with astonishment as the certainty upon which they had built their lives crumbled beneath them.

For there before them was the Son of Man, the one who had been concealed from the beginning and preserved in the presence of the Most High. The rulers who had demanded that others bow now fell upon their own faces. Those who had shown no mercy cried out for mercy, and those who had made themselves objects of fear discovered what it was to stand trembling before a power they could not command.

Then judgment came upon those who had oppressed the children of God. The vision was terrible: darkness covered their faces, angels carried them away, and the sword of judgment fell upon the violence by which they had ruled. I did not rejoice in the blood I saw, for anyone who gazes long upon suffering and learns to love suffering has allowed darkness to enter him.

Yet neither could I mourn the ending of oppression as though oppression deserved to live forever.

Then my eyes turned toward those who had spent their lives bowed down. The saints and the elect lifted their faces. They rose from the dust where kingdoms had pressed them, and the Lord of Spirits clothed them with the garment of life.

No moth would consume that garment. No age would wear it thin. Their glory would not diminish with the passing of years, for they would dwell with the Son of Man, eating and resting and rising in his presence forever.

And then I remembered the cords.

Perhaps this was what heaven had been measuring all along—not how much land a king possessed, nor how high his monuments rose, but the hidden dimensions of righteousness. The rulers of the earth had measured their greatness by what they could seize. The Lord of Spirits measured his kingdom by what could never finally be lost.

And before him, I had learned, none shall perish.
