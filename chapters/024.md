## 24 - The Waters Beneath the Earth

After I had spoken with Noah, he was shown the angels of punishment standing ready over the hidden waters beneath the earth. These were not the rivers that wander beneath trees, nor the springs from which shepherds drink, but ancient waters imprisoned in the deep, pressing against boundaries established before the memory of man. The angels waited for the command to open them, and Noah understood that the trembling beneath his feet was only the beginning.

The waters would rise for judgment upon those who remained upon the earth. No hand of man could close what heaven opened, and no fortress could stand against a sea released from beneath creation itself. The Lord of Spirits commanded the angels concerning what was to come, and when Noah had heard these things, he departed from my presence carrying both the terror of the vision and the promise I had spoken over him.

Then the word of God came to Noah.

“Your portion has ascended before me,” the Lord said, “a portion without fault, beloved and upright.” Though the earth had filled itself with violence, one life had not vanished among the multitude. Heaven had seen him—not because Noah possessed armies or treasures, but because amid a generation bending toward corruption, his heart had remained turned toward what was good.

The Lord spoke of the work the angels would soon undertake and then gave Noah a promise greater than the destruction surrounding him. “I will put my hand upon it, and preserve it. The seed of life shall arise.” The dry land would not remain empty forever; after the waters had passed, life would rise again, and Noah's descendants would spread over the earth beneath the blessing of the Lord.

Remember this when you hear of the flood, for destruction was not the final word spoken over the earth. Beneath the roar of the waters there remained a seed. Judgment moved across creation, yet within judgment God preserved the possibility of beginning again.

Then Noah was shown the fate of the angels who had corrupted humankind. They would be bound in the burning valley I had once seen toward the west, that dreadful place surrounded by mountains gleaming with gold and silver, iron, tin, and flowing metal. I remembered those mountains well: beautiful from afar, terrible when approached, their treasures shining above a valley prepared for judgment.

The ground there churned as though something beneath it struggled to rise. Waters boiled among the rocks, and from beneath the soil came the heat of hidden fire. Sulphur filled the air until every breath tasted bitter, and through the valley ran rivers that seemed to carry flame instead of water.

There the angels who had seduced the inhabitants of the earth would be confined.

I wondered at the strangeness of what Noah was shown, for those same waters were also spoken of as waters of healing. Kings and princes, the exalted and those who possessed the earth, would seek them for the relief of body and soul. They would come desiring restoration while remaining unwilling to release the very things that had wounded them.

Their bodies would feel the heat of the springs, but their hearts would cling to revelry. Day after day the signs of judgment would stand before them, yet they would refuse to believe. They had trusted the appetites of the flesh as though desire itself were a throne, and every pleasure a servant commanded to satisfy them.

There is a sorrow deeper than punishment in this. A man may stand beside healing water and still refuse to be healed. He may feel the wound within himself, recognize the fire his own choices have kindled, and yet grasp the thing that burns him because he cannot imagine life without it.

Then I heard that no word uttered before the Lord of Spirits is empty. Nothing disappears merely because men cease to remember it. Words of mercy, words of violence, promises whispered in darkness, commands given from thrones—all remain before the One whose memory does not fade.

The waters themselves would change when the rebellious angels were judged. Their heat would alter, and afterward the springs would become cold, even frozen, as though creation itself were bearing witness to what had taken place. What had burned would become still.

Then I heard Michael, the holy one, speak.

He said that the judgment of those angels would become a testimony against kings, princes, and all who possessed the earth. The same ancient sickness could be found in both: power received as gift had been turned into domination. The angels had possessed knowledge beyond men and used it to corrupt them; rulers possessed authority over men and used it to exalt themselves.

And still the waters were called waters of healing.

That mystery remained with me.

I had seen fire beneath the valley. I had smelled sulphur rising from troubled waters and heard judgment pronounced over angels and kings. Yet woven through the vision were words of preservation, healing, change, and a seed of life that would remain when the devastation had passed.

Perhaps this is why one who walks in light must never learn to love destruction, even when judgment is necessary. We may long for oppression to end without longing for the oppressor to be eternally wounded. We may rejoice when chains are broken without becoming fascinated by the fire that breaks them.

For the Lord who judges is also the Lord who preserves the seed.

And when the earth appears empty, he already knows where life will rise again.
