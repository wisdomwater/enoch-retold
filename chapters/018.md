## 18 - When the Mountains Melted

Then I saw the earth rejoice.

The mountains moved as I had never seen mountains move. They skipped like rams released into an open field, and the hills leapt like young sheep filled with milk, as though the oldest foundations of the world had suddenly become young again. What had seemed immovable was caught up in joy.

The faces of the righteous shone with it. Their long waiting had ended, and the Elect One was exalted before them. They walked upon an earth no longer possessed by those who had made it groan, and I understood that salvation was not merely an escape from creation but creation itself rejoicing in freedom.

Then, without warning, a whirlwind seized me.

The joyful earth disappeared beneath me as I was lifted and carried violently toward the west. Wind roared around my body, and the horizon turned beneath my feet until I was brought again into a place where hidden things were revealed. There my eyes encountered mountains unlike any mountains I had known.

One was iron.

Another was copper.

Beyond them stood mountains of silver and gold, and then a mountain of flowing metal and another of lead. They rose with terrible solidity, vast monuments of the substances by which men measure wealth, forge weapons, build kingdoms, purchase loyalty, and protect themselves from those they fear.

“What are these things?” I asked the angel who traveled beside me.

He told me that they stood beneath the dominion of the Messiah, the Elect One who would command upon the earth. Then the angel of peace told me to wait, for what seemed mysterious would soon be made plain.

I looked again at those mountains.

They seemed eternal.

A man may hold a piece of iron in his hand and feel its strength. He may fill his chambers with gold and imagine himself secure. A king may surround his body with armor and his city with weapons, and after many years of being obeyed he may begin to mistake the strength of metal for the strength of his own soul.

But then the Elect One appeared.

Before him the mountains began to fail.

Iron, copper, silver, gold, lead—all that had seemed permanent weakened before his presence. I watched them collapse like honeycomb held before flame, their strength dissolving as easily as water running down a mountainside. What men had trusted more than righteousness could not remain solid before him.

Then I understood why the angel had shown them to me.

Gold would not save.

Silver would not purchase escape.

Iron would no longer decide which nation survived, and armor would no longer make the violent secure. Even the substances that seemed immune to decay would become useless when the hour of the Elect One arrived.

I had seen men spend their lives gathering what they hoped would protect them from uncertainty. Wealth against hunger. Weapons against enemies. Walls against strangers. Authority against helplessness. Yet each protection creates another fear, for the one who possesses much must then fear losing what he possesses.

The mountains showed me where that road ends.

Afterward I was brought before a deep valley whose entrance stretched wide across the land. Gifts and treasures flowed toward it from those dwelling upon continents, seas, and islands. Offerings were poured into it without end, yet the valley was never filled.

I knew this valley.

Not because I had seen its equal upon the earth, but because something like it can open within the human heart.

There is a hunger that food cannot satisfy and a poverty that gold cannot cure. When desire becomes master, everything placed within it disappears. More land, more wealth, more praise, more power—still the valley whispers, *More.*

And while some gathered, others suffered.

I saw the labor of ordinary people devoured by those whose hands practiced iniquity. Men worked while others consumed the fruit of their work. The powerful became fat upon what they had not planted, and the valley widened beneath them.

Then my eyes fell upon figures dwelling near that place.

They were angels of punishment.

Before them lay terrible instruments being prepared, and when I saw them I turned to the angel of peace. There was something strange in asking an angel of peace about instruments of judgment, but by then I had begun to understand that heaven's peace is not indifference toward those who are crushed.

“For whom are these prepared?” I asked.

“For the kings and the powerful ones of the earth,” he answered.

I trembled.

I do not delight in such visions. Anyone who learns to enjoy the suffering of another should fear what has begun growing within his own heart. Yet neither could I call it mercy for oppression to continue forever simply because ending it would trouble the oppressor.

There is a false peace that asks the wounded to remain quiet so that the comfortable need not be disturbed.

That was not the peace I saw in heaven.

The peace of the Lord of Spirits meant that the righteous would finally be relieved from those who tormented them. It meant that the hands devouring the labor of others would be opened. It meant that the mountains of wealth and weaponry upon which violent kingdoms depended would no longer determine the destiny of the earth.

Then I turned and saw another valley.

This one burned with fire.

Its depths glowed beneath a darkness filled with heat, and toward it I saw monarchs and mighty men being brought. These were rulers before whom multitudes had once bowed, men whose commands had crossed borders and whose names had made nations tremble.

Now their crowns could not help them.

As I watched, I remembered the mountains skipping like lambs.

How strange that the same vision should contain both images: creation dancing and kings descending toward judgment. Yet perhaps they belonged together. The earth could rejoice because what oppressed it was ending.

I tell you, therefore, be careful what you make into a mountain.

Possessions are useful until you ask them to save you. Strength may protect until you begin worshiping strength. Even righteous anger can become another mountain if you cling to it long enough and imagine that your hatred will heal the world.

Only one kingdom in my vision required no iron to defend it.

I saw gold melt.

I saw weapons become useless.

I saw the mighty discover that domination had never made them strong.

And beyond them all, I saw the hills begin to dance.

For when the false mountains finally melted, the earth itself was free to rejoice.
