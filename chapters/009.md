## 9 - The Stars That Left Their Courses

I continued beyond the fountains of the deep, where fires descended through the pillars of heaven beyond all numbering. They seemed suspended between worlds, descending neither fully into the depths nor rising toward the heights, as though I had reached a boundary where the familiar order of creation began to dissolve. Beyond them I saw a place unlike any country through which I had traveled. There was no firmament above it, no solid earth beneath it, no water, no bird crossing its emptiness—nothing by which a man might know where he stood.

The place was desolate.

Within that emptiness I saw seven stars. They were not distant points of light such as you see scattered across the night, but immense blazing forms, like mountains set on fire. They seemed almost alive before me, like spirits lamenting their condition, and the sight of them awakened both fear and pity within me.

The angel beside me told me that this was their prison.

They had transgressed the command of the Most High, leaving their appointed courses before the proper time. I remembered the lights I had once watched rising and setting in perfect order, each appearing in its season without trespassing against the command it had received. These had refused that order. They had gone forth before their hour, and now the wandering they had chosen had ended in bondage.

There is a strange freedom in remaining faithful to one's course. I had once imagined boundaries only as walls, but the farther I traveled into the hidden places of creation, the more I saw that some boundaries preserve life. A river without banks becomes a flood. Fire outside its place becomes devastation. Even a star, abandoning its appointed path, may become a wandering flame in darkness.

Then Uriel spoke to me concerning the angels who had joined themselves to women. Their rebellion had not remained their own. They had led human beings into corruption, and men had begun bowing before powers that were not God, offering worship to things that could neither create them nor give them life. Thus the disorder spread outward, as ripples spread across water after a stone has fallen.

Uriel told me that judgment awaited these things.

I saw only a likeness of their end, not the whole mystery of it. Even now I hesitate to speak as though I understood everything shown to me, for visions may be clear in what they reveal while remaining vast in what they conceal. I can tell you only this: what oppresses, deceives, and corrupts will not be permitted to endure without end.

I, Enoch, saw these things.

No other man had stood where I stood or looked upon the end as it was shown to me. Yet I say this without boasting, because there was nothing in those places that made me feel important. The farther I traveled, the smaller I became.

I was not alone.

There were holy Watchers who remained faithful to their places. I learned their names and something of the work entrusted to them: Uriel, who walked with me through places of terror; Raphael, appointed over the spirits of men; Raguel, concerned with judgment and the heavenly lights; Michael, guardian of virtue and commander among nations; Sarakiel, who watched over the spirits of those who transgressed; and Gabriel, appointed over mysteries of paradise and the cherubim. They possessed great authority, yet unlike those who descended upon Armon, they did not grasp beyond what had been entrusted to them.

This, too, became part of what I was being shown.

Power does not corrupt merely because it is power. The holy ones were powerful beyond any king I had known, yet their greatness was expressed through faithfulness. They watched, guarded, guided, and obeyed. Their strength did not require them to abandon their station in order to prove that they were free.

Uriel then led me farther.

Again I entered a place where creation seemed unfinished. No magnificent heaven stretched above me, and no established earth rested beneath my feet. The desolation itself seemed prepared for some terrible purpose.

There I saw the seven stars again, bound together like burning mountains.

I could no longer contain my questions.

“For what crime have they been bound? Why have they been brought here?”

Uriel looked upon me and answered that these were the stars that had transgressed the command of the Most High. They would remain bound until the appointed measure of their transgression had been completed.

From there we entered another place.

Fire blazed before me, glittering with a brightness that wounded the eyes. Within it was a division like an opening torn into creation itself, and columns of flame plunged downward, struggling together as they descended into an abyss whose bottom I could not see. I searched for some measure by which to understand its depth, but there was none.

I could not discover where the fire began.

I could not discover where it ended.

“How terrible is this place,” I cried. “How difficult to explore.”

And perhaps that cry was wiser than many answers I had once desired.

There are depths before which the proper response is not explanation but trembling. There are mysteries of judgment I have seen that I will not make smaller simply so they may fit comfortably within the human mind. I have stood at the edge of the abyss and discovered that I could not measure it.

Yet even there I remembered the stars that still traveled faithfully across the heavens.

Above this prison, beyond these wandering fires, countless lights remained upon their courses.

So when you walk through a world where some have abandoned every boundary, do not believe that faithfulness has become meaningless. You need not follow every wandering light merely because it burns brightly. You need not leave your course because others have left theirs.

Some lights blaze because they are lost.

Others shine because they have remained where love placed them.
