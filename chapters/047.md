## 47 - The Sea Beneath His Hand

I saw the holy angels descend into hidden places. No chamber concealed from human eyes was concealed from them, and they gathered those who had strengthened the hands of wickedness, even those who had stood behind others and imagined themselves innocent because another had delivered the blow. Then the Most High arose for judgment, and the righteous were entrusted to the holy ones, to be guarded as tenderly as the eye guards its own sight.

In those days the wise understood what wealth had never been able to teach them. Silver could purchase walls but not refuge from truth; gold could command servants but could not command the rain to fall. Men who had spent their lives mastering the earth discovered that creation had never belonged to them.

I heard the Holy One summon witnesses.

The sun had seen what men performed beneath its light. The moon had watched deeds committed beneath darkness, and the stars had kept their silent vigil over generations that believed themselves unseen. Even the clouds, the snow, the dew, and the rain seemed to stand as witnesses against those who had used the gifts of creation to strengthen oppression.

Can you bribe the rain, my children?

Bring your silver before the clouds and ask them to open. Lay gold upon the ground and command the dew to descend. Offer jewels to the winter wind and ask it to turn aside, and you will discover how small the treasures of men become before things they did not create.

Therefore I said to the children of earth: Look carefully at heaven.

Consider what hangs above you every day without requiring your understanding. The windows of heaven open, rain descends, seeds break beneath the soil, and rivers are renewed. We receive these things so often that abundance makes us forget wonder, until we begin to behave as though the world owes us its gifts.

Then I was shown the sea.

I watched vessels venture across its face, their sails full and their holds heavy with everything their masters possessed. Those who commanded them stood confidently while the waters were calm. Yet when the winds rose, the ships climbed the dark backs of waves and plunged into the valleys between them, timbers groaning while sailors clung to whatever their hands could grasp.

Their confidence vanished quickly.

Men who commanded others could not command a single wave. Their treasures were aboard with them, yet silver could not quiet the wind, nor could authority forbid the ocean to swallow a ship. Standing before that vastness, even powerful men remembered fear.

And I wondered: if sailors tremble before the sea, why does the human heart not tremble before the One who made it?

The Most High set boundaries for those waters and placed sand along their edges. The ocean roars against the shore, yet it does not cover the whole earth merely because it possesses the strength to do so. The fish within it, the winds above it, the creatures moving across earth and sea—all exist within an order no king devised.

Then I saw even the lights of heaven tremble before the Great Glory.

The earth shook. The angels hastened to fulfill what had been commanded of them, and before that Presence even beings far greater than I desired to conceal themselves. What, then, is the pride of a man who boasts because his barns are full?

Yet my words were not given only to make sinners afraid.

I turned toward the righteous, especially those whose lives had ended without receiving the goodness their deeds deserved. I saw them descend toward the place of the dead through sorrow and lamentation, and I heard the mockery of those who had prospered through wickedness.

“They die as we die,” the sinners said. “What did righteousness profit them? They suffered, they grew weak, and now darkness receives them as it receives us. Where is their reward? Where is their light?”

I understood the cruelty of that question because I had seen righteous people die.

There are moments when the world itself seems to ask it. The gentle suffer beside the violent. The truthful enter the grave beside deceivers, and death appears to erase every distinction between the hand that healed and the hand that wounded.

But I had traveled farther than the grave.

So I said to the righteous: do not measure the worth of your life only by what was placed into your hands before death. Do not conclude that goodness has failed because wicked men enjoyed good days while you knew sorrow. I have seen that death is a boundary, but I have not seen that it is the end of the Holy One's remembrance.

Wait, therefore, with patient hope.

You need not become cruel because cruelty sometimes prospers. You need not grasp because those who grasp sometimes fill their barns. Let the wicked mistake abundance for victory if they choose.

Remember instead the sailors upon the sea.

The proud man believes himself master while the waters are calm. Wisdom begins when he feels the first great wave rise beneath him and remembers that he has always been carried.

And beneath every wave, beyond every storm, and even beyond the darkness of death, all creation remains beneath the hand of the Most High.
