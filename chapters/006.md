## 6 - Before the Throne of Fire

I stood before the Watchers with their faces veiled and their weeping quiet around me, and I began to tell them what I had seen. My tongue was only flesh, my breath the common breath given to the children of Adam, yet the Mighty One had placed words within that frail vessel. I understood then that speech is a strange gift: invisible thought becomes breath, breath becomes sound, and what is hidden in one heart may enter another. So I spoke to beings who had once inhabited heaven, though I myself was only a man of earth.

“I have written your petition,” I told them. “I carried your supplication before the Holy and Great One. But in the vision given to me, I was shown that what you ask will not be granted.” They would not ascend again to the heights they had abandoned, and before their binding they would witness the fall of the sons they loved; afterward their prayers would become silence and tears.

I did not rejoice to speak these things. There is a kind of righteousness that secretly enjoys standing above the condemned, but I had seen too much to mistake that feeling for holiness. I had been permitted to carry their prayer, and I had carried it faithfully. What happened to that prayer belonged to God.

Then I told them of the vision.

At first there were clouds.

Mist gathered around me until the familiar world disappeared, and through it I saw stars trembling as though shaken from their ancient stillness. Lightning flashed across the darkness. Winds seized me—not winds such as bend trees or drive dust across a field, but mighty currents that seemed to have been waiting for me—and they lifted me from the earth.

Higher I was carried.

The mountains fell away beneath me. The land became distant, and the heavens that men gaze upon as though they were a roof opened before me like a country without end. I was pressed onward by lightning and restless stars until at last I came to a wall unlike anything fashioned by human hands. It appeared to be built of crystal stones, clear and terrible, surrounded by flame that moved and quivered as though the fire itself were alive.

Fear entered me.

Still, I passed through the flame.

Beyond it stood a great habitation formed of crystal. Its walls shone; its pavement shone; even the ground beneath me seemed transparent with a purity that made me aware of every shadow within myself. Above me the roof moved with the appearance of stars shaken in a storm, lightning coursing among them, and cherubim of fire appearing within that terrible brightness.

The doorway burned.

I entered.

There are things of heaven for which your world has no words, so I must borrow opposites and place them beside one another. The dwelling was hot as fire and cold as ice. I felt no comfort there, no familiar warmth of hearth or field or human companionship. Majesty filled the place so completely that I trembled until my strength left me, and I fell upon my face.

Then I looked again.

Beyond the first habitation stood another, far greater than the first. Its entrances opened before me from within a sea of vibrating flame, and its magnitude overwhelmed every measure by which I had ever judged distance or greatness. Had you asked me its height, I could not have answered. Had you asked its breadth, I could only have told you that my mind could not contain what my eyes were seeing.

Its floor burned with fire. Lightning and agitated stars moved above it, and the roof itself appeared to blaze.

Then I saw the throne.

It rose before me with the brightness of frost beneath the morning sun, encircled by a radiance like the great orb of day. I heard the voices of cherubim, and from beneath the throne rivers of fire poured outward—living torrents of flame flowing from the place of divine majesty. I tried to look upon it, but there is a point where sight itself becomes helpless.

Upon the throne sat the Glorious One.

His garment was brighter than the sun and whiter than snow. Fire surrounded him, and another vast fire rose before him so that even the heavenly beings could not approach. Myriads upon myriads stood in his presence, yet none could penetrate the mystery of his face.

I could not look up.

Understand what I learned there, reader: the nearer I came to the Holy One, the less desire I had to claim that I understood him. Men speak easily about God when God is distant. We arrange words, defend boundaries, pronounce judgments, and sometimes imagine that our thoughts have enclosed the Eternal.

Before that throne, all such confidence vanished from me.

Yet something happened that frightened me more deeply than the fire.

He called my name.

“Approach, Enoch.”

Among myriads of heavenly beings, beyond walls of crystal and rivers of flame, the One whose brightness no angel could endure knew the name of a man from the dust. I had expected holiness to make me disappear. Instead, holiness summoned me nearer.

I covered my face and trembled.

Then he raised me.

I came as far as the entrance, unable still to lift my eyes. The throne blazed before me, the rivers of fire continued their terrible course, and the hosts of heaven remained around him.

But my eyes stayed upon the ground.

For I had discovered that the light we seek is greater than comfort, greater than certainty, greater even than our ability to comprehend it. To walk toward it is not to become mighty.

It is to become small enough to hear your name when the Eternal speaks it.
