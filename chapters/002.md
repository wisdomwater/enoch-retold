## 2 - The Order of Heaven

Look upward, and you will understand why my heart trembled when I first began to see. I watched the lights of heaven travel their appointed roads, each rising when its hour had come and sinking when its journey was complete. Never did I see the sun grow impatient with the morning, nor the stars abandon their stations because another path seemed brighter. They moved as though listening to a voice older than the heavens themselves, obeying a command spoken before any human mouth had learned to speak.

Then I turned my eyes toward the earth, and I saw the same hidden obedience written everywhere. Summer came, and winter followed; clouds gathered over the dry places, rain descended, and dew appeared silently upon the grass before dawn. Waters moved through rivers toward the sea, and the sea received them without overflowing the boundaries appointed for it. Creation seemed vast beyond imagining, yet beneath its wildness I sensed an order, as though every living thing rested within the hand of the One who lives forever.

I watched the trees especially. Their leaves brightened, faded, curled, and fell, until branches once heavy with green stood naked against the winter sky. Some trees kept their covering while others waited through the cold, but none mistook the season; each knew when to release and when to receive again. Standing among them, I wondered whether a tree understands surrender better than we do, for it does not clutch the leaf whose season has ended.

When summer returned, the sun burned fiercely upon the land. Stones became hot beneath my feet, the earth hardened, and men searched for shade because the open ground seemed filled with fire. Yet even this belonged to the rhythm I had been shown. The same branches that had stood empty clothed themselves again in green, fruit swelled where blossoms had been, and what had seemed dead revealed that life had merely been waiting.

I tell you these things because I began to perceive a sorrowful difference between the earth beneath us and the hearts within us. The rivers followed their courses, the stars kept their paths, and the trees surrendered themselves to the seasons, but humanity resisted the One who gave us breath. We were the creatures who had been given understanding, yet we used understanding to grasp, accuse, boast, and wound. Our mouths could bless the Majesty who made the heavens, yet from those same mouths came contempt.

There are hearts that become like branches refusing spring. They wither inwardly until bitterness seems natural to them, and because they have forgotten peace within themselves, they scatter unrest wherever they walk. I saw that such darkness cannot endure forever. Judgment is terrible, but sometimes judgment is simply the moment when what has been hidden can hide no longer, when violence meets its boundary and pride discovers that it cannot command the universe.

Yet I was also shown another people. Light rested upon them, not the harsh light of conquest, but the quiet radiance of those who had learned humility. Wisdom was given to them because they no longer needed wisdom as a weapon against their neighbors. They inherited peace because they had ceased struggling to possess everything, and their years stretched before them like an evening field beneath a gentle sun.

Remember this when the world appears ruled by those who shout the loudest. Mountains outlive kings. Rivers continue after armies have disappeared. Seeds wait beneath ruined fields, and the stars keep their ancient courses while generations of proud men rise, boast, threaten, and return to dust. The order of God is deeper than the disorder you can see.

But there are beings who can abandon even the order of heaven.

I learned this when the sons of men multiplied upon the earth and daughters were born among them, beautiful in form and radiant with the life given to mortals. From the heights, certain sons of heaven looked down upon them. These were not men gazing from neighboring hills, but beings who belonged to another realm, watchers of things below. Desire entered them, and instead of allowing desire to pass through them like wind through branches, they seized it and gave it a home.

“Come,” they said among themselves. “Let us choose wives from the daughters of men, and let us beget children.”

Their leader was Samyaza, and even he felt the shadow of what they intended. He feared that the others might abandon their purpose and leave him alone to bear the weight of the transgression. There was still, even then, a doorway through which they might have turned back. A moment comes before many acts of darkness when the heart knows what it is about to do; I have learned to regard that moment as a mercy.

But the others answered his fear with an oath.

Two hundred bound themselves together. They swore that none would retreat, and they strengthened their resolve with curses upon themselves should any abandon the undertaking. Imagine the silence of heaven as those words were spoken: beings who had watched the stars obey their courses choosing together to leave their own. What began as desire became agreement, agreement became oath, and oath became descent.

Then I saw them coming down.

The order of heaven remained above them—the stars moving, the seasons turning, the rivers running toward the sea. Yet two hundred sons of heaven descended toward the world of men, carrying within themselves a choice that would wound the earth for generations.

And I understood that darkness does not always begin with hatred.

Sometimes it begins when we see something beautiful and decide that, rather than receive its beauty with open hands, we must possess it.
