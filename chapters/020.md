## 20 - When Heaven Trembled

After these things I saw another army approaching, chariots filled with men and borne upon the winds. They came from the east, from the west, and from the south, and the sound of their wheels rolled ahead of them like thunder. I could hear their coming long before I could comprehend the multitude that filled the horizon.

Then the earth began to shake.

The pillar of the world trembled from its foundation, and the sound of the approaching host passed beyond the boundaries of the earth until it seemed that heaven itself had heard it. The holy ones above perceived the agitation below, and before the Lord of Spirits they fell upon their faces and worshiped. Thus ended the second parable I had been given.

Then I began the third.

I spoke first to the saints and the elect, for after so many visions of armies, violence, judgment, and kingdoms collapsing, I had been shown something concerning their end. “Blessed are you,” I said, “for glorious is your portion.” You have lived through the night, but you were not created for darkness.

I saw the saints dwelling in light like the light of the sun. The elect walked in the brightness of everlasting life, where their days could no longer be counted because no ending approached them. They had sought light while darkness surrounded them, and what they sought had become their habitation.

Peace was upon them.

There is something I wish you to understand about this light. Those who sought it had not always lived where light was easy to find. They had walked through an age of violence, watched the arrogant prosper, endured wounds, and carried questions that received no immediate answer.

Still they sought.

That seeking mattered.

The darkness around them had whispered that righteousness was foolish, that mercy was weakness, that only the violent inherited the earth. Yet they continued to turn toward the Lord of Spirits, and slowly righteousness rose among them like the sun approaching the horizon.

At first there is only a line of brightness.

Then the shapes hidden by night begin to appear.

Then suddenly the whole earth is morning.

So it was with the light I saw. Darkness passed away, and the light of uprightness increased before the Lord of Spirits. No one counted how long it would remain, for time belongs to things that end, and this light possessed no evening.

Then I was shown again the secrets hidden within creation.

I saw the places of lightning and the splendours from which it came. I watched brightness leap across the heavens, branching through clouds in an instant, and I learned that even lightning had an appointed purpose. It could come with judgment, yet it also came with blessing, awakening the earth and bringing fertility.

I heard thunder above me.

Its sound was like stones grinding together in some enormous chamber beyond the clouds. It rolled across the habitations of the earth, and I felt its vibration within my body. Yet I was told that thunder too could speak peace and blessing as well as warning.

Creation was stranger than the simple divisions of my mind.

The same sky could send rain upon a field and terror into the heart of a traveler. Fire from heaven could appear dreadful while preparing the earth for abundance. I began to see how little wisdom there is in judging everything only by how it first feels to us.

Then came the vision I still tremble to remember.

It was in the five hundredth year of my life, in the seventh month, on the fourteenth day. I looked upward, and the heaven of heavens began to shake.

Not the earth.

Heaven.

It shook violently.

The powers of the Most High were stirred, and thousands upon thousands of angels moved in great agitation. Myriads beyond numbering trembled around the throne, while at the center of that unimaginable disturbance the Ancient of Days remained seated in glory.

Terror entered me.

My knees failed beneath me. Strength departed from my body, my inward parts seemed to dissolve, and I fell upon my face. I had believed that after all I had witnessed I had learned how to stand before heavenly mysteries, but there are sights before which no experience can make a man strong.

I could not endure it.

Then a hand took hold of me.

Michael had been sent.

The holy angel lifted me from the ground, and as he raised me my spirit returned. I drew breath again, though my body still trembled, and I looked upon him with the helplessness of a child awakened by thunder.

“Why are you disturbed by this vision?” Michael asked.

I almost wondered how such a question could be asked. Heaven itself had convulsed before me. Powers greater than kingdoms were shaken, and I had seen beings before whom men would collapse in terror themselves trembling before what approached.

Then Michael spoke words I did not expect.

“Until now has been the day of mercy.”

Mercy.

That was the word spoken to me while heaven shook.

“The Lord has been merciful and longsuffering toward all who dwell upon the earth.”

I carried those words within me.

We often think mercy must look gentle. We imagine quiet water, soft rain, a hand laid upon a wounded head. And mercy can indeed look like these things.

But I began to wonder whether patience itself can reach an appointed end because mercy also belongs to those who are being harmed.

How long should the oppressed be told to wait?

How long should violence be permitted to feed upon the weak?

How long can darkness be allowed to call God's patience permission?

I do not know the measure. I am not the Judge.

That knowledge has kept me humble.

I tell you only what I saw: the Ancient of Days upon His throne, heaven trembling around Him, and Michael speaking of the long mercy that had preceded that hour.

There are times when the person of light must remain patient.

There are also times when light must expose what darkness has hidden.

Wisdom is knowing that neither hatred nor fear should decide between them.

When heaven shook, I fell upon my face. Perhaps there was wisdom even in that. When mysteries become greater than our understanding, we need not pretend to stand taller than we are.

Sometimes the holiest thing a human being can do is tremble.

And wait for a hand to lift him.
