## 49 - The Child of Light

I swear to you, children of righteousness, that your goodness is not lost. I saw the angels recording it before the glory of the Mighty One—not only the deeds that men praise, but the quiet acts no earthly witness remembers. What is hidden from the world is not hidden from heaven.

Therefore wait with patient hope. I know there are seasons when this is difficult, when those who prosper through oppression appear stronger than those who refuse it. I saw the righteous disgraced and afflicted while sinners flourished, and I understood how easily the heart begins to wonder whether integrity is merely another name for weakness.

But I saw what awaited them.

The righteous shone like the luminaries of heaven. Gates that had seemed closed opened before them, and the cries they had uttered beneath oppression were heard. An account was required not only from those who had plundered them, but also from princes and from all who had strengthened the hands of their oppressors.

So I said, Do not join yourselves to oppression merely because oppression prospers. Keep your distance from it. Better to stand among the forgotten in the company of heaven than among the celebrated whose greatness requires another person to remain beneath their feet.

Nothing is as hidden as men imagine.

Day and night behold us. Light and darkness pass over the deeds of every generation, and the heavens themselves seem to bear witness to what occurs beneath them. A man may perform evil beneath the covering of night, but darkness is not blindness to God.

Guard therefore the word of uprightness.

I was shown a strange mystery concerning words themselves. I saw generations arise in which men would speak falsehood boldly and fashion writings according to their own desires. They would undertake great works with words, altering truth until what was crooked seemed straight.

Yet I saw my own words traveling beyond me.

They crossed tongues I had never spoken and entered the hands of peoples whose faces I had never seen. Scribes wrote what I had uttered, and others rendered the words into their own languages. I desired that nothing be changed or diminished, for a word entrusted to another is like a flame carried through a strong wind: it must be guarded if it is to arrive still burning.

Then I saw books given to the righteous.

They were books of joy, integrity, and wisdom, and those who received them rejoiced in what they discovered. The words did not exist merely to fill the mind with mysteries, but to reveal paths upon which feet might walk. Wisdom that never enters the manner of one's living remains like a lamp kept covered.

And I heard the promise of the Lord.

The children of earth would be called to listen, and wisdom would not remain sealed among the heavenly ones. The Holy One would dwell in communion with those who walked uprightly, and peace would be given to the children of integrity. Truth would become not merely something spoken, but something inhabited.

I thought this might be the final wonder shown to me.

But after these mysteries my sight returned to the generations of my own house.

My son Mathusala had taken a wife for his son Lamech, and in time she conceived. Nothing seemed strange in this, for children had been born beneath our tents since the days of our fathers. Yet when the appointed hour came and the child emerged from his mother's womb, those who saw him were seized with wonder.

His flesh was white as newly fallen snow, yet within it was the color of the rose. Hair white as wool fell long upon his head. He seemed unlike the infants we had held before, as though some brightness from another realm rested upon him.

Then the child opened his eyes.

Light filled the house.

It spread across the walls like sunrise entering a chamber all at once, until every corner seemed touched by it. Those gathered there stared in astonishment, for the brightness appeared to come from the child himself, and the house overflowed with radiance.

I had spent my years looking upward toward lights beyond the reach of men. I had watched stars travel their appointed paths, seen gates of heaven opened, and stood trembling before beings clothed in fire. Yet now the mystery appeared in another form—not above the earth, but within a newborn child.

And I remembered what I had told the righteous.

Wait with patient hope.

For darkness makes one believe that the future can contain only more darkness. Oppression looks upon itself and imagines it will live forever. But God hides beginnings where the mighty do not think to search for them—in faithful words preserved through generations, in quiet lives recorded by angels, and sometimes in the helpless body of a child.

I did not yet understand everything this birth would mean.

I only knew what I saw.

After visions of judgment, after generations of violence, after so many warnings concerning fire and destruction, a child opened his eyes.

And the house filled with light.
