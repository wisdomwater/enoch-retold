## 36 - The Ship Above the Waters

Four figures like white men descended from heaven while the earth still cried beneath the violence of the beasts. Three of them came to me and took hold of my hands, lifting me away from the generations I had been watching. They carried me to a high place, so high that the hills below seemed to shrink into folds upon the earth, and there before me stood a lofty tower.

“Remain here,” they told me. “Watch what comes upon the elephants, the camels, the asses, the stars, and all the cattle.”

So I watched.

The first of the four went toward the star that had first fallen from heaven. He seized it, bound it hand and foot, and cast it into a valley so deep and narrow that its darkness seemed to have substance. The fallen light disappeared into that terrible hollow, and I understood that even beings who descend from heaven cannot escape the boundaries established by the Lord forever.

Then another of the white ones drew a sword and gave it among the monstrous creatures. The elephants, camels, and asses turned upon one another, striking and tearing until the earth shook beneath their violence. What they had inflicted upon others now consumed them from within; the power by which they had terrorized the earth became the instrument of their own destruction.

Then the great stars were gathered—the ones who had left their places above and taken forms like horses among the creatures of earth. The angel bound them also, hand and foot, and cast them into cavities beneath the ground. One after another the fallen lights vanished into darkness until the heavens were emptied of their rebellion.

I did not rejoice.

There are judgments so terrible that silence is the only fitting response. I had longed for the violence to end, but when its ending came, I found no sweetness in destruction. It is possible to desire the liberation of the oppressed without learning to delight in the ruin of the oppressor.

Then one of the four approached the white cattle and revealed a mystery to them.

One of the cattle trembled, and before my eyes it became a man. He began to build an enormous vessel, shaping it according to the knowledge he had received. When it was finished, he entered it, and three cattle entered with him, hidden beneath its covering.

I looked upward.

Above the earth appeared something like a vast roof, and within it seven cataracts opened. Water poured downward with a roar so great that the air itself seemed to tremble. At the same time fountains broke open beneath the ground, and water surged upward from the depths.

Heaven and earth had opened together.

The waters rose until the village disappeared. Fields vanished, trees sank, and the boundaries by which men had divided one piece of land from another were erased beneath the flood. Darkness covered the waters, clouds gathered above them, and still the rain fell.

I watched the cattle struggle.

The elephants, camels, asses, and all the creatures that had filled the earth disappeared beneath the rising deep. There was nowhere to flee. The strength that had made some creatures terrifying upon dry land could not save them when the world itself became water.

Only the ship remained.

It floated upon the darkness, small beneath the enormous heavens yet held above the abyss. I remembered the prayer I had prayed long before: *Leave a seed upon the earth.*

There it was.

Not an army. Not a fortress built upon an unshakable mountain. A vessel floating helplessly upon waters no human strength could command.

Sometimes preservation looks very much like surrender.

At last the cataracts of heaven closed. The fountains beneath the earth became quiet, and other depths opened to receive the waters. Slowly the flood withdrew, the ground appeared again, and the ship came to rest upon the earth.

Then the darkness receded.

Light returned.

The white creature who had become a man stepped out, and the three cattle came with him. One was white, another red as blood, and another black. The first white one departed from them, and once again the earth began to fill with life.

But the world that emerged from the waters was not yet a world without violence.

Wild creatures multiplied—lions and wolves, dogs and boars, foxes and birds of many kinds. They gathered across the earth, and soon they began biting one another. The flood had washed over the world, but something restless remained within the creatures who survived it.

Among them, however, another white bull appeared.

From the generations that followed came a white sheep, and that sheep brought forth twelve sheep. I watched them grow together until one of the twelve was taken away and delivered into the hands of asses. The asses then gave him to wolves.

I feared for him.

A sheep has little with which to defend itself among wolves. It possesses neither their teeth nor their hunger for domination. Yet the sheep lived among them and grew there, surrounded by creatures stronger than itself.

Then the Lord did something I did not expect.

He brought the other eleven sheep into that same land.

Together they dwelt among the wolves, and there they multiplied. Pasture was given to them in abundance, and their number increased until what had begun with one vulnerable creature became a people.

As I watched them grazing beneath the shadow of wolves, I began to understand something of the strange way God preserves life.

He does not always remove the sheep from the place where wolves dwell.

Sometimes he causes them to grow there.

The world after judgment was still unfinished. Teeth remained. Violence remained. Darkness had not vanished forever simply because the waters had receded.

Yet neither had the light.

So do not believe that you must become a wolf merely because wolves surround you. The sheep survived without growing fangs of their own. The ship survived not by overpowering the flood, but by being carried through it.

And when the waters finally withdrew, the first thing heaven gave the wounded earth was not certainty.

It was light.
