## 48 - Written in Heaven

I had heard the mockery of those who looked upon the graves of the righteous and believed the matter settled. They saw the good descend in sorrow to the dwelling of the dead and said, “What did their goodness profit them?” For the grave appears from this side to close over every story alike, and those who judge only by what their eyes can see may easily believe that death has spoken the final word.

But I had been shown what lies beyond the sight of ordinary men.

Therefore I swear to you, my children, by the splendor of the Great One and by the majesty of His kingdom: I learned this mystery. I saw the tablet of heaven. I read what the holy ones had written upon it, and there, before the throne of the Mighty One, I discovered that the names of the righteous had not disappeared into darkness.

Goodness had been prepared for them.

Joy had been written beside their sorrow, and glory beside the humiliation they had endured. What had been withheld from them in the land of the living was not therefore lost. I saw that their portion would exceed what they had known beneath the sun, and that the spirits of those who died in righteousness would awaken to rejoicing.

Their remembrance remained before the Mighty One from generation to generation.

Consider what this means, my children, for I have watched men labor desperately to make themselves remembered. They carve their names into stone, raise monuments, accumulate treasures, and seek honor from generations they will never meet. Yet I saw names praised upon earth disappear like writing traced in dust, while lives scarcely noticed by men remained inscribed in heaven.

Then I looked upon those whom the world had called fortunate.

Some had lived surrounded by wealth until their final day. They had escaped the troubles that crushed their neighbors, and no sword had entered their houses. They died upon comfortable beds with honor surrounding them, and those who stood nearby said, “Blessed were they. See how well they lived, and how peacefully they have gone.”

But death does not turn falsehood into truth.

I saw that the deeds a man carries into darkness become companions there. What had been hidden beneath prosperity could no longer be concealed by it. Wealth remained behind, titles remained behind, servants and houses remained behind, but the shape the soul had taken through its choices passed with it beyond the boundary.

And then I heard another cry.

It came from those who had lived beneath oppression.

“We were diminished,” they said. “Our spirits were consumed. Day after day we scarcely expected to live. We labored and were devoured by those stronger than ourselves. Their yoke rested heavily upon our necks.”

Their voices multiplied until they seemed to rise from every generation.

“We searched for somewhere to flee, but found no refuge. We went to princes and cried out for protection, yet those who possessed the power to help us would not listen. Instead they strengthened the hands of those who plundered us.”

I heard them speak of oppression hidden carefully from sight. Their strength was taken from them little by little, their lives consumed while those responsible concealed what they had done. Some were slain, and even their slaughter was covered over, as though silence could erase blood from the earth.

Their cries pierced me.

There is a particular darkness in suffering beneath those who could show mercy but choose not to. Yet darker still is the hour when the wounded begin to wonder whether anyone has seen them at all. When rulers refuse to hear, neighbors turn away, and heaven itself seems silent, despair whispers that perhaps the suffering has vanished into nothing.

But I had seen the tablet.

That is why I speak to you.

The cries that princes ignored had reached farther than the courts of princes. The lives diminished on earth had not been diminished before God. Every hidden burden, every neck bent beneath an unjust yoke, every prayer uttered when there seemed nowhere left to flee—all had entered the remembrance of the Mighty One.

So do not measure a life by the ease of its ending.

Do not call the oppressor blessed because he dies surrounded by gold, nor call the righteous forgotten because they die with tears upon their faces. The grave may conceal both from your sight, but it does not erase the distinction between them.

And if you are among those whose goodness seems to have brought little reward, guard your heart against despair. Do not allow the cruelty done to you to become the pattern by which you begin treating others. The suffering you endured is real, but it need not become the seed of another person's suffering.

I cannot tell you that every wound will be healed before you leave this earth.

I can tell you what I saw.

Beyond the reach of kings, beyond the houses of the wealthy, beyond the courts where cries went unheard, there stood a tablet in heaven.

And upon it, the righteous were remembered.
