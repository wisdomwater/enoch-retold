## 15 - When Wisdom Found No Home

I continued to look upon the habitations of heaven, and there I saw those who had denied the Lord of glory being drawn away from the dwelling of the holy. Yet as I watched, no blow descended upon them from the Lord of Spirits. This mystery remained before me: they were removed from the place they had refused, and their separation itself seemed already a judgment.

Then other chambers of creation were opened to my sight. I saw the secrets of lightning and heard the hidden places from which thunder receives its voice. I was shown the winds before they scatter across the earth, the dew before it settles upon the grass, and the clouds before they gather over mountains and fields.

There were great receptacles prepared for these things, hidden storehouses beyond the sight of men. I saw the places of hail and snow, and the chambers from which clouds emerged. I even beheld that ancient cloud which had hovered over the earth before the ordering of the world, as though creation still remembered its first morning.

Nothing seemed ordinary to me after this. A man hears thunder and thinks only of the storm above his roof; he wakes and finds dew upon the ground and gives little thought to it. But I had been shown that behind these familiar things stood mysteries, pathways, and appointed places, and I began to understand how little of creation we truly see.

I was shown also the receptacles and pathways of the moon. I watched its coming forth, its journey, its fading and glorious return, increasing and diminishing while never abandoning the course appointed to it. The moon kept faith with its path as though bound by an ancient oath.

Night after night it obeyed.

There was something beautiful in that obedience. The moon did not grasp after the brilliance of the sun, nor did it refuse the darkness through which it must travel. It received its season of fullness and its season of emptiness, and in both it remained faithful to the command of the Lord of Spirits.

I saw its hidden path and its visible path, its movement by day and by night. The lights of heaven turned continually toward their Maker, magnifying Him without weariness. Praise was their rest.

Consider this mystery, you who dwell upon the earth. We often imagine rest to mean the end of movement, yet I saw heavenly bodies moving without ceasing and nevertheless resting in God. Perhaps the deepest weariness does not come from movement but from resistance—from the heart forever struggling to become what it was not given to be.

The Judge beheld all these things, and nothing escaped His sight. Light and darkness stood before Him, and so too did the spirits of men. No angel could alter His seeing, for judgment belonged finally neither to angels nor to men, but to the One before whom every hidden thing becomes known.

Then I was shown something that grieved me more than storms or darkness.

Wisdom had sought a dwelling among the sons of men.

I saw her as one seeking shelter, moving through the habitations of humanity and looking for a place where she might remain. She came near our houses, our councils, our markets and places of power, but no dwelling was prepared for her. At last Wisdom departed from the earth and returned to heaven, taking her seat among the angels.

After she had gone, iniquity came seeking the same thing.

And iniquity found room.

She did not need to plead for entrance. She settled among humanity like rain falling upon the desert, like dew sinking eagerly into thirsty ground. What Wisdom had searched for and could not find, iniquity received almost without asking.

This vision pierced me.

For darkness does not always conquer by great violence. Sometimes it needs only an empty chair. Sometimes evil enters because truth was inconvenient, mercy demanded too much, patience seemed too slow, and wisdom knocked quietly while another voice promised an easier road.

Yet heaven had not forgotten the earth.

I lifted my eyes and saw again the splendour of the stars. The Lord of Spirits called each by name, and they heard Him. Their brightness, their places, the times of their appearing, and the courses of their turning were weighed in a righteous balance; splendour came forth from splendour until the darkness itself seemed seeded with light.

I asked the angel beside me what these things meant.

He told me that the Lord of Spirits was showing me a likeness of the righteous who dwell upon the earth and trust in His name. Then I looked again at the stars differently.

They were surrounded by darkness, yet darkness did not make them dark.

A star does not defeat the night by becoming greater than the night. It simply burns where it has been placed. Its light may seem small against the enormous heavens, yet from distances beyond imagining it can still be seen.

Then another parable opened before me, and I heard of the Elect One.

A day would come when the Elect One would sit upon a throne of glory, and those who had sought refuge in the holy name would behold him. Their spirits would be strengthened at the sight of him. Countless habitations would be prepared, and the Elect One would dwell among them.

Then I heard words that astonished me: heaven itself would be changed.

The face of heaven would be illuminated and blessed, and the face of the earth would also be changed. The righteous would dwell upon it in peace. The world wounded by violence, grasping, accusation, and blood would not simply be abandoned; the vision spoke of the earth itself receiving blessing.

But evil would not be permitted to rule there forever. The Lord had seen the paths of those who practiced oppression, and judgment approached them. This was not merely anger cast into creation without purpose; it was the refusal of God to allow what destroys His creatures to endure without end.

I remembered Wisdom wandering homeless among us.

Perhaps the renewal of the earth begins when a dwelling is prepared for her again.

So make such a place within yourself.

Do not imagine that you must illuminate the whole night. You are not the sun, and you were never commanded to become it. Be instead like one of those stars I saw: know the path entrusted to you, remain faithful to the light you have received, and refuse to let the surrounding darkness determine what you become.

For I saw splendour arise from splendour.

I saw light calling forth light.

And I learned that darkness, however vast it appears, cannot teach a faithful star to cease shining.
