## 43 - The Weeks of the World

My children, I speak now concerning the children of righteousness, concerning those who choose integrity when another road lies open before them. I do not speak from conjecture, for I have seen visions beneath the heavens and heard the voices of the holy ones, and the tablets above were opened before me. Yet the more I was permitted to know, the more I understood how little a man can hold of the mysteries of God.

I saw the generations of the world stretched before me as a succession of weeks. I was born in the first, the seventh from Adam, while righteousness waited patiently and judgment had not yet revealed its hour. Time itself seemed to me like a great river whose distant bends were hidden from those standing upon its banks, though from heaven its whole course was known.

In the second week I saw wickedness rise and fraud spring from the earth like thorns after rain. The world that had been young grew violent, and the thoughts of men became corrupted until judgment came upon them and the waters covered what they had built. Yet humanity was preserved through the ruin, for even when judgment passes through the earth, God does not forget the seed from which another beginning may grow.

Then came the third week, and from among humankind one was chosen from whom the plant of righteous judgment would grow. I saw that plant take root through generations, fragile beside the empires surrounding it, yet carrying within itself a purpose stronger than their armies. God often entrusts what will endure to beginnings the mighty scarcely notice.

In the fourth week visions were given to the holy and righteous, and an order was established among the generations. In the fifth I saw a house of glory rise, a dwelling dedicated to the Great King. Its stones appeared strong, and men might have believed that what had been raised for God could never fall.

But the sixth week darkened.

Wisdom faded from hearts that had once treasured it. The house itself was consumed by fire, and the people of the chosen root were scattered like seed cast by a violent wind across distant lands. I watched smoke climb where songs had once risen, and I learned that possessing a holy place does not make the human heart holy.

The seventh week brought a crooked generation whose works multiplied according to its crookedness. Yet even there the everlasting plant had not died. The righteous were gathered from it and entrusted with wisdom concerning the works of creation, as though light, driven underground through an age of darkness, had broken through the soil again.

Then I saw an eighth week, a week of righteousness. Judgment was placed into the hands of the righteous, and the power of oppressors was broken. Habitations of peace were established, and the house of the Great King became a place of rejoicing.

After this came the ninth week, and righteousness was revealed throughout the world. The works of ungodliness faded from the earth, and humanity began searching for the path of integrity. What had once seemed hidden among a few became visible to many, like dawn spreading from one edge of heaven until the shapes concealed by night could be seen.

Then I beheld the tenth week.

On its seventh day judgment reached even the ancient Watchers, those mighty ones whose rebellion had wounded the earth in ages before my birth. No height of being placed them beyond the Holy One. The old heaven itself passed away, and before my astonished eyes another heaven opened—vast, spacious, alive with powers shining sevenfold brighter than those I had known.

Beyond it I saw weeks without number, unfolding in goodness and righteousness. Sin itself ceased to be named. I could scarcely understand such a world, for we who have lived among violence become so accustomed to guarding ourselves against darkness that we struggle to imagine existence when there is nothing left to fear.

And there I became silent.

For who among the children of men can hear the voice of the Holy One and remain unmoved? Who can contain His thoughts? We lift our eyes and see the workmanship of heaven, but seeing is not comprehending.

Look upward on a clear night, my child. You may count a few stars before their multitude defeats you, but do you know the chambers in which their fires rest, or the paths appointed for their rising? You walk upon the earth, but have you measured its breadth, or stretched a cord across heaven to discover its height?

A man may speak about the heavens without having ascended into them. He may behold the movement of creation without perceiving the spirit by which it lives. I myself was carried beyond places forbidden to ordinary sight, and still I returned knowing that mystery was greater than my vision.

Therefore do not mistake knowledge for mastery.

I showed you the weeks not so that you might possess the future, but so that you might know how to walk through the present. Kingdoms rise and houses burn; wisdom is forgotten and found again; generations become crooked, and righteousness appears buried beneath them. Yet through all these weeks the Holy One does not surrender the world.

Choose, then, the path of righteousness. Do not walk the roads of oppression merely because many feet have made them broad. Keep far from the paths that require another person to be diminished so that you may become great.

For I have seen where all the roads lead.

The road of domination ends, however mighty its travelers appear. But the path of integrity continues beyond the falling of kingdoms, beyond the burning of houses, beyond even the passing of heaven as we know it.

Walk that path, my children.

Choose righteousness, and choose life.
