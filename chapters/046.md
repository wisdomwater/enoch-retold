## 46 - When Falsehood Becomes Sacred

There came before me a vision of an age in which men did not merely speak falsehood, but honored it. They adorned deception with beautiful words and gave authority to what was crooked, changing the words of integrity until injustice could wear the garments of righteousness. Then those who had committed no evil were cast down, and innocent heads were pressed into the dust beneath the feet of those who had learned to make lies appear holy.

Yet above them another testimony was being gathered. I saw the prayers of the righteous rise into the presence of heaven, carrying with them griefs that earthly judges had refused to hear. The angels received them and remembered them before the Most High, and I understood that a truth rejected on earth does not cease to be true.

Then the vision darkened.

Nations trembled and families were torn apart. I saw mothers fleeing with children against their breasts, only to lose them in the terror that overtook the land. Infants were abandoned before they could be instructed in the ways of their fathers, and bonds that should have been stronger than fear were broken by the violence spreading everywhere.

In that confusion men reached desperately for powers they had fashioned themselves. They carved stone and wood, shaped silver and gold, and bowed before the work of their own hands. Temples filled with images, and men sought help from spirits and idols that could neither hear their sorrow nor deliver them from it.

I watched them kneel before stone.

There was something grievous in this beyond the foolishness of worshiping what human hands had made. Their hearts had become so blinded that they preferred a god they could shape to the Holy One before whom they themselves must be shaped. A stone does not ask a man to become merciful; gold does not rebuke oppression; an idol fashioned by our hands can always be made to approve the desires of the one who fashioned it.

But blessed were those who received wisdom.

I saw them walking through that troubled age without joining themselves to its madness. They sought the path of the Most High when other roads promised easier passage, and they refused to become impious merely because impiety surrounded them. Their salvation was not that they escaped every sorrow, but that darkness failed to make them its own.

This is a mystery worth guarding, my children. You cannot always choose the age into which you are born, nor can you prevent every violence committed around you. But you can guard the place within yourself where you decide what you will worship and what kind of creature you will become.

For I saw how wickedness spreads.

One man enlarged the crime of his neighbor rather than healing it. Another laid a foundation of deceit, and another built upon it, until entire houses rose from the labor and suffering of others. Brick followed brick and stone followed stone, and those dwelling comfortably within them ceased to ask whose bent backs had carried the weight.

I pronounced woe upon such houses.

A dwelling may be beautiful to the eye and still contain misery in its foundations. A kingdom may boast of its greatness while its stones cry out against it. Whatever is built by making another human being less than human already carries within itself the beginning of its collapse.

Then I saw what happens when violence is allowed to feed upon itself.

Fathers fell beside their children, and brothers died beside brothers. The hand that should have protected became the hand that struck, until even the ancient tenderness of kinship could no longer restrain bloodshed. From the rising of the sun until its setting the slaughter continued, and blood gathered upon the earth like a terrible river.

Horses waded through it to their breasts.

Chariot wheels sank toward their axles.

I wished to turn my eyes away, but the vision was given to me that I might witness where hatred leads when no one will release it. Violence promises mastery, yet it eventually devours every boundary meant to contain it. Once men have taught themselves that another life may be destroyed for the sake of their cause, they soon discover new enemies nearer and nearer to home.

Therefore do not feed the darkness you wish to overcome.

Do not alter the words of integrity because truth has become inconvenient. Do not worship the work of your own hands simply because it never contradicts you. Do not build your peace upon another person's misery, nor magnify the wrongdoing of your neighbor because condemnation makes you feel righteous.

Receive instead the word of wisdom.

Walk the path of the Most High even when the multitude moves elsewhere. Keep mercy alive when cruelty becomes ordinary, and truth when falsehood is praised. Let no age become so dark that it persuades you darkness is light.

For I saw prayers rising above the slaughter.

I saw angels remembering what men had forgotten.

And I learned that when falsehood becomes sacred among men, the quietest act of truth may itself become an offering before heaven.
