## 40 - The Horn That Would Not Break

The seasons of the shepherds continued, each receiving the flock for an appointed time and then passing it into the hands of another. Thirty-seven came and completed their periods, and others followed them. I watched generations of rulers appear and disappear while the sheep remained below, enduring the consequences of those appointed over them.

Then I lifted my eyes, and the sky darkened with wings.

Birds of prey came from every direction—eagles, kites, ravens, and others whose forms I had been shown. An eagle seemed to lead them, and together they descended upon the sheep. Their wings beat against the air as they fell upon the flock, tearing flesh and pecking out eyes.

The sheep screamed.

I screamed with them.

Though I lay asleep while seeing these things, I groaned aloud against the shepherd who stood watching while the flock was consumed. Dogs attacked from below while birds descended from above. They tore the sheep until neither flesh nor skin remained, and their bones lay scattered upon the ground.

The flock grew smaller.

More shepherds came and completed their appointed periods. Still the destruction continued, until I wondered whether anything would remain of the people I had watched cross the waters and enter the good land.

Then something small appeared.

Lambs were born.

They seemed insignificant beside the eagles circling above them, yet these lambs began to do something many of the older sheep could no longer do.

They opened their eyes.

They looked around and cried out to the flock, as though trying to awaken those who had forgotten how to see. But the older sheep did not answer. They had become deaf as well as blind, hardened by long wandering until even the voices of their own young could scarcely reach them.

The ravens noticed the lambs.

They descended upon them, seizing and tearing them. Yet as I watched, horns began to grow upon some of the young sheep. The ravens landed upon those horns and fought against them, and suddenly from among the flock there rose one horn greater than the others.

It grew large and strong.

The eyes of the creature bearing it opened wide.

He looked upon the sheep and cried out.

Others who could still see gathered around him, even while the birds continued carrying away the flock. Ravens struck at him. Eagles and kites circled above. The powers that had grown accustomed to feeding upon blind sheep now discovered among them one who could see.

They tried to break his horn.

They could not.

Again and again they came against him. The shepherds joined the birds and urged them onward: “Break the horn. Fight against him. Kill him.”

Still he stood.

But he did not stand silently.

He cried out for help.

That cry mattered.

For I saw the keeper of the book—the one who had recorded every sheep destroyed and every excess committed by the shepherds. He came down from the presence of the Lord, and this time all could see him descending to the aid of the one whose horn the ravens could not break.

Then the Lord of the sheep himself came in wrath.

Everything changed before his presence.

Those who had seemed invincible fled. Creatures that had fed without restraint fell before him. The birds gathered with the beasts and made one final attempt against the great horn.

But the books were opened.

The recorder brought forward the account of the last shepherds and showed how greatly they had exceeded what had been permitted. Every slaughter they thought forgotten remained written there.

The hour of accounting had come.

I saw the Lord take the sceptre of his wrath and strike the earth. The ground split open beneath the beasts and birds, and those who had preyed upon the sheep fell into the depths. Then the earth closed over them.

Afterward a sword was given to the sheep, and they went against the beasts that remained.

This vision disturbed me.

I had watched the sheep suffer so long that part of me wanted to see their enemies scattered. Yet I also remembered how easily a sheep may become like the creature it fights. I had already seen one ram gain power and lose his glory.

So I kept watching.

For judgment was not complete merely because the oppressed had been given strength.

The beasts fled.

Then, in a beautiful land, I saw a throne erected.

The Lord of the sheep sat upon it.

And before him were brought the sealed books.

There, at last, I understood why the records had been kept so carefully. Judgment would not belong to the anger of the wounded or the appetite of the powerful. The books were brought before the Lord.

He opened them.

Nothing was missing.

Every shepherd who exceeded his authority, every beast that devoured, every sheep that disappeared beneath violence—all had been remembered.

Then the Lord summoned the first seven white ones and commanded that the ancient rebels be brought before him, beginning with the first star that had fallen from heaven.

The judgment was reaching backward through generations.

I watched the throne and trembled.

For there is comfort in knowing that evil will not reign forever, but there is also humility in knowing that the final reckoning does not belong to us.

When darkness surrounds you, you may be called to stand.

You may be called to cry out.

You may even be called to resist what devours the innocent.

But do not confuse resistance with the throne.

The great horn did not save itself. When the birds gathered to break it, it cried for help.

And help descended.

Perhaps courage is not the certainty that we are strong enough to defeat every darkness. Perhaps it is the willingness to keep our eyes open when others have closed theirs, to stand when others have surrendered, and to cry toward heaven when our own strength has reached its end.

For I saw the horn surrounded.

I saw the ravens strike it.

I saw the shepherds demand that it be broken.

And I saw that it did not break.
