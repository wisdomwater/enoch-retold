## 50 - The Child Called Noah

When the child was lifted from the hands of the midwife, another wonder came upon the house. He opened his mouth, though he had only just entered the world, and blessed the Lord of righteousness. Lamech heard the voice of his newborn son and fear seized him, for the child seemed to him less like one born of mankind than one of the beings who had descended from heaven.

He fled to Mathusala and told him everything. “His eyes are like rays of the sun,” Lamech said. “His face is glorious, and when I look upon him I cannot believe that he belongs to me.” The memory of the Watchers still lay heavily upon our generations, and Lamech feared that their ancient transgression had appeared again beneath his own roof.

Mathusala therefore came searching for me.

At that time I dwelt far away, at the extremities of the earth, among mysteries few men had been permitted to see. Across that great distance I heard my son's voice crying for me. I went to meet him and said, “Behold, my son, I am here.”

He told me of the child—skin white as snow and red as the rose, hair like pure wool, eyes whose opening flooded the house with light. He told me how the infant had blessed the Lord of heaven while still in the midwife's arms. Then he repeated Lamech's fear: perhaps this was not truly his son, but some offspring of the heavenly ones.

I understood why Lamech trembled.

I had seen what happened when the boundary between heaven and earth was violated. In the days of Jared, the Watchers abandoned the station appointed to them, took women for themselves, and brought forth children of violence. Their rebellion had not remained in heaven; its wound spread into the earth until corruption seemed to seep through every generation.

And now judgment was approaching.

I told Mathusala what I had seen written upon the tablets of heaven. A great flood would pass over the earth, washing through a world swollen with violence. The destruction would be terrible, and what men believed permanent would disappear beneath the waters.

“But this child is Lamech's son,” I told him.

There was no deception in his birth. The brightness surrounding him was not the sign of another transgression, but the sign of a preservation. When the waters came, this child would survive, and with him his three sons would remain alive upon the earth.

“Call his name Noah.”

For through him consolation would come to a wounded world.

Mathusala's fear softened into understanding, and he returned to Lamech carrying my words. The child who had frightened his father by seeming too strange to belong to him was, in truth, his own son. Sometimes what God sends for our preservation first appears to us as something we cannot understand.

Yet I did not tell Mathusala that Noah would end all darkness forever.

The tablets had shown me otherwise. Generations would rise after him, and transgression would rise with them. Evil could be washed from the face of the earth, yet the mystery of the human heart remained deeper than floodwaters.

Still, I had seen farther.

Generation would follow generation until a righteous people arose, until transgression was exhausted and goodness filled the earth. I did not understand every road by which creation would reach that hour. I knew only that the Holy One had not abandoned His purpose because mankind repeatedly wandered from it.

Later I was shown again what becomes of those who give themselves wholly to corruption.

Before me appeared something like a cloud, black and impenetrable, so deep that I could not see through it or discover what lay beyond. Within it burned a terrible brightness. Mountains of flame seemed to turn and collide within the darkness, and from its depths came cries of anguish.

I asked the holy angel beside me, “What is this place?”

He told me it was appointed for those who perverted what God had spoken, those who chose evil and strengthened themselves in falsehood. Their deeds too were written above. Heaven remembered not only the cries of the humble but also the hands that had caused those cries.

Yet even while I looked upon that fearful place, my thoughts returned to the child.

Noah.

A newborn surrounded by light while judgment gathered beyond the horizon.

This is one of the mysteries I have carried with me: God showed me destruction, but He also showed me preservation. He showed me the abyss, but before the waters ever rose He placed a child in Lamech's arms. Judgment had not yet fallen, and already the seed of another beginning was breathing upon the earth.

Therefore, when you behold an age darkened by what human beings have done, do not suppose that darkness alone is growing.

Somewhere a seed may already have been planted.

Somewhere a word may already have been spoken.

Somewhere, unseen by those who believe violence owns the future, God may already be preparing the vessel that will carry life through the storm.

I had seen the flood coming.

But first, I saw a child open his eyes.

And the house filled with light.
