## 14 - Beneath the Wings of the Lord

I saw the dwelling of the holy beneath the wings of the Lord of Spirits. How shall I speak of such a thing without making it smaller by my words? They stood before Him like living flames, countless faces bright as fire, and from their mouths came blessing upon blessing, rising together until praise seemed no longer something they offered but the very air in which they lived.

Righteousness dwelt there continually. It did not come and go as it does among us, appearing for a season and then disappearing beneath greed, fear, or violence. It remained before Him like an everlasting river, and beneath those great wings there was no striving for place, no grasping after honor, no fear that another's brightness might diminish one's own.

My soul longed to remain there.

I had traveled through deserts and over mountains. I had stood near prisons prepared for rebellious spirits, seen valleys appointed for judgment, crossed regions where stars moved through the gates of heaven, and looked upon things that made my bones tremble. But there, beneath the wings of the Lord of Spirits, something within me became still, as though after wandering through a long night I had suddenly remembered my home.

I knew that place as an inheritance before I understood how I knew it. Something within me recognized what my eyes had never before seen. Perhaps every soul carries some faint memory of the shelter for which it was made, and this is why nothing we possess beneath the sun can completely quiet our longing.

I remained gazing upon that habitation for as long as I was permitted, blessing the One whose knowledge was before the foundations of the world. Before mountains rose, before seas were gathered into their places, before the first bird opened its wings beneath the heavens, He knew. Generations appear and disappear like grass beneath the seasons, but the knowledge of the Lord of Spirits has neither morning nor evening.

“What is this world?” I wondered as I stood there.

I thought of kings guarding their treasures, armies marching beneath banners, merchants counting silver, and men quarreling over boundaries drawn upon soil. All these things had seemed so solid when I walked among them. From the heights of heaven they appeared like marks traced briefly upon water.

Then I heard those who do not sleep in the dust. They stood before the glory of God crying, “Holy, holy is the Lord of Spirits,” and their voices seemed to fill the world beyond the world. Thousands upon thousands surrounded Him, then myriads upon myriads, until number itself failed me and I understood that I was looking upon a multitude no mortal tongue could count.

The glory became more than my eyes could endure. My face changed beneath its brightness, and I could no longer look directly upon what stood before me. There are mysteries hidden because we are unworthy of them, but there are others hidden because they are too great for the vessel that receives them; even a good cup will overflow when a river is poured into it.

Yet before my sight failed, I perceived four great presences upon the four sides of the Lord of Spirits. Their voices rose distinctly from the multitude, each carrying something different before God. The angel who accompanied me knew their names, but I did not yet ask him, for first I listened.

The first voice blessed the Lord of Spirits forever. It was strong and peaceful, possessing no trace of fear, though it sounded before the throne of immeasurable power. The second blessed the Elect One and remembered those who suffer because they belong to the Lord of Spirits, and in that voice I heard the sorrow of wounds that heaven itself had not forgotten.

The third voice prayed for those who dwell upon the earth.

This moved me deeply, for I had seen how small the earth appeared from the heights. I had watched its mountains shrink beneath me and its rivers become threads, yet heaven did not forget those tiny creatures walking upon its dust. Their cries rose farther than they knew.

The fourth voice was terrible. It drove away the impious angels and forbade them to enter the presence of the Lord in order to accuse those who dwell upon the earth. I heard the force of that command roll outward, and I understood that accusation does not possess the final word before God.

Then I turned to the angel of peace who traveled beside me.

“Who are these four,” I asked, “whose voices I have heard?”

He answered me, “The first is Michael, the merciful and patient, the holy one. The second is Raphael, who presides over every suffering and every wound of the sons of men. The third is Gabriel, who presides over all that is powerful. And the fourth is Phanuel, who presides over repentance and the hope of those who shall inherit eternal life.”

I pondered their names and the ministries entrusted to them.

Around the throne of immeasurable glory I had expected only power. Yet I found mercy there. I found one who attended to wounds, another who prayed for humanity, and another who guarded repentance and hope.

This taught me something about the strength of heaven.

The world often calls a man powerful when others fear him. Kings display power by crushing resistance; armies display it by breaking walls; the angry display it by making others tremble. Yet beside the Lord of Spirits I saw a different greatness: patience beside power, healing beside judgment, intercession beside holiness, and hope standing guard over the road of return.

After this, further secrets of heaven were opened to me. I saw the divisions of paradise and the habitations prepared for the elect and the holy. I saw also the deeds of human beings brought forth and weighed as though upon balances, nothing lost, nothing forgotten, every hidden movement of the heart exposed before a wisdom that could not be deceived.

And I saw sinners who had rejected the Lord of glory being driven from that place.

I trembled, for the vision would not permit me to pretend that our choices are weightless. What we do matters. What we become matters. Every act of mercy, every cruelty hidden from human eyes, every truth spoken at cost, every wound inflicted because we desired power—all of it enters into the great mystery of what a soul is becoming.

Yet even as I watched the balances, I remembered the four voices.

I remembered Raphael standing near every human wound. I remembered the voice praying for those still wandering upon the earth. Above all, I remembered Phanuel, guardian of repentance and hope, standing before the everlasting throne.

So do not imagine that walking in the light means never having wandered into darkness. The path of return remains a holy thing. When you recognize darkness within yourself, do not cherish it, but neither surrender yourself to accusation; turn again toward the light while turning is still given to you.

For I stood beneath the wings of the Lord of Spirits and discovered that heaven is more terrible than men imagine.

But it is also more merciful.
