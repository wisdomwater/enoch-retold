## 32 - When the Moon Goes Dark

Uriel kept me beneath the courses of the great lights, for there was still more that I had not understood. I had watched the moon grow from a thin brightness until its face became round and luminous, but now the angel showed me the mystery of its receiving and its surrender. Its light was not its own possession; it was given, gathered little by little in the presence of the sun, until after fourteen days the moon stood full in the heavens.

I watched it rise from the east as the sun descended into the west. All through the night it traveled above the sleeping earth, pouring silver over mountains, rivers, trees, and the roofs of men, until dawn appeared and the greater light rose before it. Then the moon faded from sight, not because it had ceased to exist, but because another glory had overwhelmed its brightness.

Uriel showed me how its fullness did not remain. The light that had entered it began to diminish, portion after portion, night after night, until at last nothing remained visible. The round body of the moon was still there, traveling its appointed path, yet to human eyes its orb had become solitary and dark.

I remember gazing into that darkness for a long while.

There are things that remain even when their light cannot be seen. Men easily forget this, for we trust our eyes too greatly. When a thing shines, we say that God has blessed it; when it disappears into darkness, we imagine that it has been abandoned. Yet I had watched the moon vanish without leaving its course, and I knew that hiddenness is not the same as absence.

Then came the first day, and light entered it again.

Uriel called this the beginning of the moon. What appeared empty was receiving what could not yet be seen, and what seemed finished had already begun again. The first brightness was scarcely anything against the vast darkness of heaven, yet the angel knew what it would become, for its measure and its appointed days were known.

So the moon waxed and waned according to its ordinances. Some months were numbered differently from others, and its course did not precisely equal the course of the sun, yet neither wandered without measure. Uriel showed me its weeks, its gates, its diminishings and renewals, until I understood that heaven contains rhythms more intricate than the reckoning of men.

Then he said to me, “Enoch, I have shown you these things.”

His words carried a weight that made me silent. I had been shown the sun and moon, the conductors of the stars, the seasons of their coming and going, and the laws by which their courses returned. I had looked upon an order so vast that the span of a human life seemed no more than a breath beneath it.

But Uriel's countenance grew grave.

He told me that days would come when disorder would spread among those who dwell upon the earth. Seed would fail in fertile ground. Rain would be withheld, fruit would not ripen in its proper season, and trees would stand barren when their branches should have bent beneath their abundance. Even the signs by which men had learned to mark the heavens would seem confused, and the familiar courses of the lights would no longer offer the certainty men expected from them.

I heard these things with dread, for I had just been shown the beauty of order. I had seen light received by measure, stars keeping their courses, seasons arriving at their appointed gates. Now I was shown what it would mean for a world to become disordered.

Yet the deepest disorder Uriel described was not in the sky.

“The thoughts of those who dwell upon the earth shall transgress within them,” he told me.

Then I understood why he had first taught me to look upward. The heavens had become a mirror before me. Sun, moon, winds, and stars received their places without claiming to be the source of their own glory, but men would forget their place and imagine themselves to be gods.

That is a terrible darkness, my children—not merely to lose the light, but to forget that it was ever given.

The moon had taught me otherwise. I had watched it become glorious without pride and become dark without despair. It received and released. It became full and surrendered its fullness. Even when no light could be seen upon its face, it remained beneath the ordinance of the One who had appointed its path.

But the human heart can grasp what was meant to be received. It can take strength, knowledge, wealth, beauty, authority, even righteousness, and whisper, *This light belongs to me.* From that small deception greater darkness grows, until a creature made to reflect glory begins to demand the place of its Giver.

Uriel warned that such disorder cannot continue forever. Evil multiplies when nothing restrains it, and judgment comes because creation cannot endlessly bear what destroys it. I trembled at this, for judgment is fearful, yet I had seen enough suffering to know that endless oppression would be fearful also.

So remember the moon when your own light diminishes.

Do not grasp at what is leaving you. Do not call yourself abandoned because you have entered a darkened season. The moon travels unseen before it shines again, and the first gift of returning light is received while most eyes still see only darkness.

Walk your appointed course. Receive what is given. Release what must depart.

And never mistake the light upon your face for the One from whom the light has come.
