## 39 - The Book of the Forgotten

After the tower burned and the house of the sheep was thrown down, I strained my eyes to see whether the flock would return to it. I could not tell. Smoke seemed to hang over the place where the house had stood, and the sheep were scattered among beasts that waited for them with open mouths.

The shepherds continued delivering them over.

Each shepherd was given his season, and each had received a number beyond which he was not to pass. Yet they killed beyond what had been commanded, as though suffering had awakened an appetite in them. Those entrusted with limits discovered that power can whisper to the heart, *No one will know.*

But someone knew.

I saw the one who carried the book.

Day after day he watched the destruction, and day after day he wrote. Every sheep destroyed by the shepherds was recorded. Every life surrendered to the beasts, every act committed beyond the appointed measure, every excess concealed beneath the language of authority—all entered the book.

And I wept.

My sorrow became anger as I watched the flock disappear. I wanted the Lord of the sheep to rise immediately. I wanted the shepherds cast down and the beasts driven away. There is a kind of anger that is born not from wounded pride but from seeing the helpless crushed, and I felt it burning within me.

Yet the Lord did not act when I wished him to act.

Instead, the writer ascended.

He carried the book into the presence of the Lord of the sheep and placed the record before him. The Lord took it into his own hands and read it. He saw every name, every number, every excess of the shepherds.

Then he sealed the book.

And he kept it.

That image remained before me: the sealed book resting in the dwelling of the Lord.

For men imagine that what is not punished immediately has been forgotten. The cruel man sleeps peacefully and thinks his cruelty has vanished with the day. The ruler buries the names of those he has destroyed beneath victories and monuments. Generations pass, witnesses die, and eventually men say, “Who remembers?”

Heaven remembers.

After this I saw the shepherds continue through their appointed hours. Then three sheep returned to the ruined place. They entered among the stones and began rebuilding what had fallen.

Wild boars opposed them.

Still they built.

Stone was placed upon stone, and slowly the house rose again. They raised the tower until once more it stood high above the flock. Where fire had consumed, hands labored; where walls had fallen, walls appeared again.

I wanted to rejoice.

Then I looked at the table.

They had placed it again before the tower, but the bread upon it was impure and unclean.

The house had returned.

The tower had returned.

The table had returned.

But something had not returned.

The sheep were blind.

Even the shepherds were blind.

Then I understood a grief deeper than ruins. A house can be rebuilt while the heart remains broken. Sacred stones can be returned to their places, ancient forms restored, songs remembered, tables prepared—and still those gathered around them may not see.

I had once watched the sheep leave bondage and begin to open their eyes in the wilderness. Now I watched them standing near a restored tower while blindness covered them.

Do not mistake rebuilding for healing.

There are ruins made of stone, and there are ruins hidden inside us. The first may be repaired by strong hands. The second requires light.

Because the shepherds could not see, they failed again in the work entrusted to them. Sheep and shepherds became confused together. Those who should have protected the flock trod upon it, and the beasts continued to devour.

Still the Lord was silent.

I struggled with that silence.

I will not tell you that every silence of God has been explained to me merely because angels have carried me through heaven. There are mysteries I have seen more clearly than I desired, and mysteries that remain hidden even after all I have witnessed. Sometimes faith is not understanding why the Lord waits; it is refusing to believe that his waiting means he has ceased to see.

For the writer was still writing.

At last he ascended again with the book.

This time he did more than present the record. He stood before the Lord of the sheep and pleaded for them. He pointed to what the shepherds had done and testified against their violence.

The keeper of the record became an intercessor.

This moved me deeply.

He had counted suffering without becoming indifferent to it. He had witnessed cruelty without allowing cruelty to harden him. He did not merely write, *another sheep destroyed*, close the book, and turn away.

He carried their suffering into the presence of God.

Perhaps this is one way a person of light survives an age of darkness.

See clearly.

Do not hide the truth.

Name what has been destroyed.

But do not allow the record of evil to become a book of hatred within you.

Carry it upward.

For I saw the book placed again beside the Lord of the sheep, and I knew that every life the shepherds had treated as expendable remained present there. The powerful had reduced them to numbers, but heaven counted them for another reason.

Not because they were forgotten.

Because they were remembered.

And somewhere in the silence of God, the book remained sealed, waiting for the hour when everything written within it would be brought into the light.
