## 8 - At the Foundations of the World

The voice of the Holy One continued, and I listened with my face lowered before the glory. He spoke of the giants whose bodies would perish while their restless spirits remained upon the earth, and of the Watchers whose judgment waited until the great consummation. Then he returned to the petition I had carried into heaven. “Say to those who sent you: you were in heaven, yet even there not every secret was revealed to you.”

These words astonished me, for I had imagined that those who dwelt in heaven must surely know all heavenly mysteries. But there are depths even angels have not sounded. The Watchers had taken hold of a forbidden mystery and, in the hardness of their hearts, had brought it into the world of men. Knowledge that had not ripened into wisdom became another source of suffering, and the earth multiplied the consequences of what they had revealed.

“Tell them,” the voice said, “you shall have no peace.”

Afterward the holy ones raised me and carried me away. They appeared surrounded by burning fire, though when they desired they could take the likeness of men. I could no longer tell whether I was traveling across the earth I had known or through some deeper country hidden beneath its familiar appearance. Distances opened before me that seemed too vast for mortal feet, and I surrendered myself to those who carried me.

We came to a mountain whose summit reached toward heaven.

There I saw chambers prepared for powers I had known only by their effects. Light had its treasury there. Thunder had its dwelling. I beheld a bow of fire and arrows held ready within a quiver, and beside them a sword fashioned of flame; lightning itself seemed stored there like a weapon waiting for the appointed hour.

Until then I had known thunder as a sound rolling over the hills and lightning as a sudden wound of brightness across the clouds. Now I saw them as though from behind the veil. Things that appear wild to us may have hidden places from which they come and boundaries beyond which they do not pass. Once again I found order where human eyes perceive only chaos.

From there I was taken to waters that spoke with their movement, a babbling stream whose voice filled the strange country around me. Beyond it burned a fire in the west where the setting sun was received. Evening itself seemed to have a doorway there, and I watched the great light of day descend toward it as it had done since the first mornings of the world.

Then I saw a river made of fire.

It flowed as water flows, yet its current was flame, moving westward until it emptied into the great sea. I stood watching it, unable to decide whether I was witnessing destruction or beauty. There are sights in the hidden creation where such distinctions become difficult; fire can consume, but it can also illuminate, purify, and warm. I did not name what I had not been given to understand.

We continued past the great rivers until light itself seemed to withdraw.

I entered a region of immense darkness, toward the places where all flesh eventually journeys. Mountains of gloom rose there, bearing the cold of winter within them. I saw the places from which waters entered the abysses, the mouths of the rivers of the world, and openings into the deep whose bottoms my eyes could not find.

The earth I had once believed I understood was becoming immeasurably strange.

Then I was shown the winds.

I saw their receptacles and perceived how they served the whole creation. The winds were not merely empty air wandering without purpose; they upheld, moved, carried, and adorned. I saw the four great winds bearing the earth and the firmament, rising between heaven and earth like unseen pillars.

Some turned the heavens. Some accompanied the courses of the sun and stars toward their setting. Others moved over the earth carrying clouds upon their backs, bearing the waters that would become rain. Through their invisible paths I also saw the paths traveled by angels.

At the extremity of the earth I looked upward and beheld the firmament stretched above it. Then my guides carried me southward, where six mountains burned day and night. Three stood toward the east and three toward the south, formed not of common rock but of glorious stones—variegated stone, pearl-like stone, antimony, and mountains red as though the earth itself had been kindled.

The middle mountain towered above them.

Its height reached toward heaven like a throne. Alabaster gleamed along its vast sides, and its summit shone with sapphire, blue and deep as a piece of heaven made solid. Over all those mountains hung a blazing fire.

Beyond them stretched another country where waters gathered together. I saw fountains of the earth descending into depths beside the fiery pillars of heaven, hidden sources from which waters emerged into the world. Everywhere I looked, something visible seemed to arise from something invisible.

I began to understand why the Watchers' sin had been so grave.

The universe is filled with mysteries, but mystery is not an invitation to possession. I had been permitted to see chambers of thunder, rivers of flame, pillars of wind, mountains of precious stone, and fountains hidden in the depths. Yet none of them belonged to me because I had seen them.

I did not need to seize the fire.

I did not need to command the winds.

I did not need to carry every secret back and turn it into power.

There is a gentler way of standing before mystery: to behold it, receive the wonder of it, and allow what remains hidden to remain hidden.

The Watchers had reached into mystery with grasping hands.

I was learning to enter it with open ones.
