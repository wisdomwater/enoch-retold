## 3 - When the Earth Cried Out

They descended upon Ardis, the summit of Mount Armon, two hundred beings of heaven standing upon the high places of the earth. I have wondered what the mountain felt beneath them, for stones remember weight even when men forget. There they had sworn their oath, binding themselves one to another with curses, and because of that terrible covenant the mountain carried their memory in its name. What should have been a place where earth reached toward heaven became the place where heaven transgressed against earth.

I learned the names of those who led them: Samyaza above them, and with him Urakabarameel, Akibeel, Tamiel, Ramuel, Danel, Azkeel, Saraknyal, Asael, Armers, Batraal, Anane, Zavebe, Samsaveel, Ertael, Turel, Yomyael, and Arazyal. Remember that darkness has names, though I do not speak them so that you may fear them. I speak them because evil becomes easier to serve when we imagine it only as a mist without faces or choices. These were beings who chose, and choice after choice carried them farther from the order they had once known.

They took women for themselves and entered into union with them, crossing a boundary that had stood between heaven and earth. But they brought more than desire down from the heights. They opened knowledge that had been hidden, teaching incantations and sorceries, revealing the powers of roots and trees and mysteries mortals had not learned through patient wisdom. Knowledge descended faster than the human heart could learn how to carry it.

Then children were born unlike the children of men.

Giants walked upon the earth, immense and terrible, their hunger as vast as their bodies. I saw fields emptied before them, storehouses consumed, and the labor of ordinary hands disappearing into mouths that could never be filled. Men planted, harvested, ground the grain, and gathered fruit, yet nothing satisfied these beings. Hunger without measure eventually devours more than food.

When the produce of the earth was exhausted, the giants turned upon humanity. Then their violence spread outward—to birds that fled through the air, beasts that ran across the fields, reptiles upon the ground, and creatures swimming beneath the waters. Flesh consumed flesh, blood was drunk, and the harmony I had once contemplated in the seasons seemed buried beneath appetite. The earth herself recoiled from what was happening upon her.

I heard it said that the earth reproved the unrighteous.

Do not imagine this as a human voice rising from the soil. It was deeper than speech. Blood had entered the ground; creatures made for life were being destroyed; the balance between receiving and taking had been shattered. Creation itself had become testimony against those who believed their strength gave them permission to consume without limit.

Azazyel added another wound. He taught men the making of swords and knives, shields and breastplates—the arts by which fear could clothe itself in iron and violence could extend the reach of the human hand. He showed them ornaments, precious stones, dyes, mirrors, paints, and ways of altering their appearance. None of these things carried evil simply by existing, yet in hearts already learning to grasp and dominate, even beauty became another means of possession, rivalry, seduction, and pride.

Others disclosed still more. Amazarak taught sorcery and the dividing of roots; Armers unfolded enchantments; Barkayal taught the observation of the stars; Akibeel revealed signs; Tamiel opened the mysteries of astronomy; Asaradel taught the motion of the moon. Things hidden in heaven were being drawn downward into a world whose wisdom had not grown alongside its power. The world changed quickly, but the hearts of men did not deepen with it.

This is one of the sorrows I have seen beneath the sun: power can grow more quickly than mercy.

At last the cries became too numerous to distinguish one from another. The cry of the mother mingled with the cry of the orphan; the terror of the hunted rose with the grief of those whose fields had been stripped bare. Blood soaked the ground, and the earth, deprived of her children, carried their lament upward. What men could no longer remedy became a cry at the gates of heaven.

Then Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Suryal, and Uriel looked down.

They saw it all.

From heaven the violence could not disguise itself with the excuses used upon earth. They saw the blood, the corruption, the suffering, and the secret knowledge that had been twisted toward destruction. The cries of human souls rose before them, asking that justice be sought from the Most High, and the holy ones carried that grief before the eternal throne.

“Lord of lords, God of gods, King of kings,” they cried. “Nothing is hidden from you.”

Those words have remained with me. Nothing is hidden.

This can terrify the one who loves darkness, but to the wounded it can become a strange kind of hope. The blow struck where no witness stood is still seen. The tears shed behind a closed door are not lost. The powerful may silence the mouths of those beneath them, but they cannot silence the earth, and they cannot build a wall high enough to prevent suffering from reaching heaven.

I had seen the stars obey. I had seen rivers remain within their courses and trees surrender their leaves when the season required it. Now I saw what happened when beings endowed with freedom refused all measure and made appetite their law.

Yet above the cries stood a throne that had not moved.

The darkness was real. The blood was real. The suffering of creation was real.

But none of it was unseen.
