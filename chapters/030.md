## 30 - The Harmony of the Heavens

I continued watching the moon as Uriel revealed its mystery to me. Night after night its brightness increased until all fourteen portions were filled, and the pale crescent I had first seen became a great circle of light suspended against the darkness. Nothing in its growth was hurried; each portion arrived when its hour had come.

Uriel showed me the law governing this wandering light, and I wrote what he taught me. I recorded the stations of the moon, the months of its journey, and the changing appearance of its face until its brightness was completed. Fifteen days carried it toward fullness, each night receiving what the night before had prepared.

Then its path continued.

The moon passed through the gates of heaven, sometimes drawing near the road of the sun and sometimes moving away from it. Through the third and fourth gates I saw the two luminaries share their courses, and then the moon traveled onward through the fifth and sixth. It went forth for seven days and returned for seven, and at other times eight days carried it farther along its appointed circuit.

I confess to you that the numbers bewildered me at first.

Uriel did not seem troubled by my slowness. Patiently he showed me the movements again, tracing before me the paths of sun and moon as one might trace streams descending from a mountain. What appeared from the earth to be wandering was not wandering at all.

So I wrote.

I wrote seven days and eight. I wrote thirty days and three hundred and sixty-four. I recorded the years and their accumulated days, the difference between the journeys of the sun and moon, and the manner in which their courses returned again toward harmony.

There were differences between them that could not simply be ignored. Over years the days accumulated differently, and Uriel taught me to notice the intervals, to count what seemed missing, and to see how the great reckoning remained ordered. Three years, five years, eight years—the numbers grew beneath my hand while above me the lights continued their journeys.

I began to understand that harmony does not require sameness.

The sun did not become the moon, nor the moon the sun. They moved differently and carried different measures of light, yet their differences belonged within a greater order. Each remained faithful to its own path, and through those differing paths the seasons continued.

Uriel then showed me four days of particular importance, servants stationed within the turning of the year. Men had become confused concerning them and lost the proper reckoning, but heaven had not forgotten. One belonged to the first gate, another to the third, another to the fourth, and another to the sixth.

Through these, Uriel said, the harmony of the world was completed.

I loved those words: *the harmony of the world.*

Until then I had often thought of heaven as a place of overwhelming power. I had seen rivers of fire and angels terrible in appearance. I had watched judgment descend and heard voices before which the rulers of earth would tremble.

But now heaven appeared to me as music.

The seasons were its rhythm. The years were its returning melody. Sun, moon, and stars moved like voices within a song too vast for any one creature to sing alone.

Uriel showed me twelve gates standing open at the extremities of heaven, and through them passed the chariots of the sun. Rays streamed outward when the gates opened, and heat spread over the earth according to its season. Through other openings came winds and the spirit of dew, each released at its appointed time.

I saw windows upon the right and upon the left. Some opened upon fierce heat. Others belonged to the stars, which went forth when commanded and returned according to their number.

Above them moved the chariots of heaven.

I watched them travel through the heights toward the gates where the stars turned. Some stars descended from human sight, while others circled without setting. Among these I saw one course greater than the rest, sweeping around the whole world like a guardian keeping its ancient watch.

And standing beneath that immense turning, I felt very small.

Yet I did not feel insignificant.

There is a difference.

A man who believes himself insignificant imagines that because he is small, he does not matter. But a man who knows himself to be small may discover the freedom of belonging to something immeasurably greater than himself.

I had seen men destroy themselves trying to become large. Kings enlarged their borders. The Watchers reached beyond the boundaries appointed to them. Men gathered forbidden knowledge because they could not endure being creatures who did not possess every secret.

The heavens showed another way.

The moon did not need the brilliance of the sun. The stars did not abandon their stations because another star appeared greater. Dew did not envy rain, nor winter struggle to become summer.

Each received its place.

Each kept its path.

And together they became harmony.

Perhaps this is why Uriel made me count so carefully. Faithfulness is not always found in extraordinary deeds. Sometimes it is hidden in returning to the same gate, receiving the portion given for that day, and refusing to despise the small place assigned to you.

I looked upward once more at the chariots moving through the heavens.

Nothing hurried.

Nothing grasped.

And still the whole sky moved.
