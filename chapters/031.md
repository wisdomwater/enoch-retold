## 31 - The Gates of the Winds

At the farthest reaches of the earth, where the familiar world seemed to loosen its hold and creation opened into mysteries beyond the sight of ordinary men, I was shown twelve gates. They stood at the boundaries of heaven, three toward the east, three toward the west, three toward the north, and three toward the south. From them the winds came forth, not wandering without purpose, but moving according to laws appointed before I was born and before any king had drawn a boundary upon the earth.

I watched them emerge from their hidden chambers, and I learned that not every wind bears the same gift. From some came blessing, health, rain, dew, fruitfulness, and life; from others came drought, scorching heat, frost, blight, destruction, and loss. The same heavens that opened to nourish the fields could also open to strip them bare, and I understood that creation itself possesses a severity that no man can command.

From one eastern gate came heat fierce enough to bend the grasses toward the dust. From another came rain and the softness of dew, and beneath it the earth seemed almost to breathe, dark soil drinking what heaven poured upon it. Yet from a neighboring gate came cold and dryness, as though abundance and barrenness stood nearer to one another than men suppose.

So it was also in the south, the north, and the west. One gate released fragrant air and life-giving rain; another sent mist, frost, snow, or blight. I saw no struggle among the gates, no rebellion among the winds. Each came forth when summoned, and each withdrew when its appointed work was complete.

This troubled me more than I first understood.

Among men we name blessing and calamity as though God must dwell only in the one and be absent from the other. We rejoice when rain falls upon our fields and ask where heaven has gone when the land becomes hard beneath our feet. Yet at the boundaries of the earth I saw that the winds of abundance and the winds of diminishment both remained within an order greater than my judgment.

Uriel led me farther, and the world widened before me. I saw seven mountains rising higher than the mountains known among men, their upper reaches pale with frost. I saw rivers stretching through the earth like immense shining cords, some turning toward seas in the east, others disappearing into distant waters beyond regions I could name. Islands rested upon the great sea, solitary beneath the heavens, while days and seasons passed over them unnoticed.

There I felt the smallness of human life very sharply. Men build walls and declare, “This belongs to me.” They measure their fields, count their cattle, store their grain, and imagine that what they possess has become secure. Yet the winds cross every boundary without asking permission, rivers outlive kingdoms, and mountains watch generations pass like shadows moving across stone.

Then Uriel showed me the great lights again, the sun and the moon, and taught me their names and their measures. I watched the moon receive its light little by little until its face became full, and then surrender that brightness portion by portion until darkness took it again. Nothing was grasped. Nothing was kept forever.

The moon did not mourn its diminishing.

That vision stayed with me. Among men, we cling to fullness as though fullness were the only sign of blessing. We fear every waning: strength leaving the body, wealth departing the household, beauty fading from the face, friends passing beyond our reach. Yet heaven itself contains increase and decrease, appearing and disappearing, gathering and release.

I do not say that suffering is therefore pleasant, nor that destruction should be called good merely because it exists beneath heaven. I have seen too much grief to speak so carelessly. But I learned that the person who would walk in light must not build his peace upon the promise that nothing will ever change.

The winds will shift.

The moon will diminish.

Seasons will pass over the mountains.

A gentle heart cannot survive by commanding these things to remain still. It survives by learning where to stand when they move.

I began to understand that surrender is not the same as helplessness. The winds obey laws hidden from us, yet within their passing the earth is watered, seeds awaken, rivers swell, snow gathers upon the heights, and life continues. Even what appears to us as loss may belong to a rhythm whose whole design we have not yet been shown.

So when darkness gathers, do not become dark in order to resist it. When the hot wind comes, do not allow your heart to become scorched with it. When frost settles over what you hoped would bloom, guard the small warmth entrusted to you.

For I have stood where the winds are released, and I tell you this: the world is larger than the season you are presently enduring.

Beyond the gate of drought there is also a gate of rain.

Beyond diminishing there is fullness again.

And above them all remains the One who has set their courses, before whom even the winds wait for their appointed hour.
