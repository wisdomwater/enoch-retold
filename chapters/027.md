## 27 - Between the Rivers of Fire

After these things, I heard the name of the Son of Man exalted among the inhabitants of the earth. His name moved among the chariots of the Spirit and passed through their midst like a sound carried upon a wind no earthly ear could hear. What had once been concealed was being spoken openly, and the hidden one who dwelt with the Lord of Spirits was no longer hidden from those appointed to behold him.

Then I was brought to a place between the north and the west. There the angels carried the cords I had seen before, stretching them across a country beyond the lands of men, measuring a dwelling for the elect and the righteous. As I watched them work, I saw the fathers of the first generations and the saints of ages past dwelling there, gathered into a country where death could no longer scatter them.

Then something happened to my spirit that I cannot fully describe.

I was taken upward and concealed within the heavens. The earth fell away beneath me, and I entered regions where the ordinary measures of distance seemed to have no meaning. There I saw the sons of the holy angels walking upon flaming fire as men walk upon a road, their garments whiter than anything woven upon earth and their faces transparent as crystal filled with light.

Before me flowed two rivers of fire.

They glittered with a strange blue brilliance, beautiful and unbearable at once. Their flames moved like living water, folding over themselves and throwing light across the heights. I had passed through many wonders by then, yet at the sight of those rivers my strength deserted me, and I fell upon my face before the Lord of Spirits.

Then a hand took mine.

It was Michael.

He grasped my right hand and raised me from the ground, as he had done before when terror overcame me. He led me farther into the heights, into the hidden places where mercy and righteousness have their secrets. I had thought judgment among the deepest mysteries of God, but now I learned that mercy also possesses depths into which angels themselves gaze.

Michael showed me the extremities of heaven and the chambers from which the stars go forth. I saw their splendors gathered like fires waiting behind unseen gates. Then my spirit was carried still higher, into the heaven of heavens, until I no longer knew whether I stood somewhere or whether I had become only sight and wonder.

There, in the midst of immeasurable light, I saw a habitation built from what appeared to be stones of ice.

They shone with a purity that pierced the eye, cold in appearance yet filled within by tongues of living flame. Fire moved through ice without consuming it. Around the habitation ran rivers of living fire, and surrounding it were Seraphim, Cherubim, and Ophanin—the sleepless ones who keep watch before the throne of glory.

Beyond them were angels without number.

Thousands upon thousands gathered there, then myriads beyond my ability to count. Their presence filled the heights like stars filling a moonless sky, yet even that image is too small. Michael, Raphael, Gabriel, and Phanuel moved among them, together with holy ones whose names I did not know.

Then I saw the Ancient of Days.

His head was white like pure wool, and his garment cannot be described in the language of men. I searched within myself for some comparison by which I might tell you what I saw, but every image seemed poor beside him. My knees failed, my flesh seemed to dissolve from me, and something within my spirit changed.

I fell upon my face.

Yet from somewhere deeper than fear a cry arose in me. I blessed him. I glorified him and exalted him with a voice that seemed stronger than the body from which it came, and I knew that my blessing had been received before the Ancient of Days.

Then he came surrounded by Michael and Gabriel, Raphael and Phanuel, and hosts beyond numbering. The sight should have driven every thought from me, yet an angel approached and spoke directly to me.

“Offspring of man,” he said, “you were born for righteousness, and righteousness has rested upon you.”

I did not feel righteous then. I felt small.

Perhaps that is what the presence of true holiness does. It does not cause a man to admire his own goodness; it strips him of comparison until he stands astonished that he has been permitted to stand at all.

“The righteousness of the Ancient of Days shall not forsake you,” the angel continued. “Peace shall be called to you.”

Peace.

After all I had seen—the Watchers descending, giants filling the earth with violence, angels imprisoned, kings trembling, valleys burning, monsters beneath the sea, and the coming judgment—the word spoken over me was not power.

It was peace.

I began to understand then that peace is not merely the absence of enemies. I had seen too much to believe such a thing. Peace comes from somewhere older than conflict, for the angel told me it had gone forth from that place since the creation of the world.

And those who walked the path of righteousness would share in that peace. Their dwelling would endure. Their portion would not be taken from them, and the path of integrity would remain beneath their feet in the name of the Lord of Spirits.

So I tell you what I learned in the heaven of heavens.

I stood among rivers of fire and beings whose faces shone like crystal. I saw the sleepless guardians of the throne and hosts that could not be numbered. I stood before the Ancient of Days until my flesh seemed to melt away.

And after all that terror and glory, heaven gave me a single word to carry back into the darkness of the world:

Peace.
