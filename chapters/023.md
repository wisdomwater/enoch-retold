## 23 - When the Sceptres Fall

I saw the kings of the earth delivered into the hands of the angels of punishment, and all the splendor that had surrounded them seemed suddenly thin as smoke. These were rulers before whom nations had trembled, men who had sat beneath canopies of gold and believed the earth itself secure beneath their feet. Yet a little rest was granted to them before judgment, and in that brief stillness they fell upon their faces before the Lord of Spirits.

Then I heard words from their mouths that they had refused to speak while their thrones were strong. “Blessed is the Lord of Spirits,” they cried, “the Lord of kings, the Lord of princes, the Lord of the rich, the Lord of glory and wisdom.” Their voices trembled as they confessed that his power passes from generation to generation, that his secrets are deeper than the mind can search, and that his righteousness cannot be measured.

The Lord illuminated what they had hidden. Secret chambers opened before him; motives concealed beneath noble words were exposed; violence committed far from watching eyes stood plainly in the light. The kings saw themselves without flatterers, without monuments, without the stories they had told about their own greatness, and at last they knew that there is a throne before which every other throne is dust.

They begged for rest, but the rest they desired could not be found. “Light has passed away from us,” they lamented, “and darkness has covered our thrones.” They remembered how they had trusted the sceptre in their hands, how they had mistaken dominion for permanence and glory for righteousness, and now the very things upon which they had leaned could not bear their weight.

I listened as they confessed that their sins were beyond numbering. Their souls, they said, had become filled with the instruments of wrongdoing, as though violence had been their food until they could no longer remember another way of living. And still their knowledge came too late to rescue the kingdoms they had built within themselves.

The Son of Man stood before them, and darkness gathered upon their faces. From his presence they were driven, and the sword remained before him as the decree against those who had possessed the earth by oppression. I trembled at the sight, for it is a fearful thing to watch a creature finally encounter the harvest of everything it has chosen.

Yet as I watched, I understood why the righteous must be careful even in the hour when oppressors fall. Hatred can kneel before the same altar as justice and disguise itself in holy garments. If the suffering of an enemy becomes sweet to us, then the darkness we condemned in him has found another dwelling place.

The Lord of Spirits needed no hatred from me to establish righteousness.

Then my sight was carried into another secret place, and there I saw unfamiliar countenances. An angel told me, “These are those who descended from heaven to earth.” They had revealed mysteries that were not theirs to give and had taught the children of men powers by which desire became domination and knowledge became an instrument of corruption.

And then I beheld Noah.

The earth beneath him had begun to lean as though its ancient foundations were failing. The ground shuddered violently, and the world itself seemed sick beneath the weight it carried. Noah saw destruction approaching and journeyed toward the ends of the earth, seeking me with terror upon his face.

“Enoch!” he cried. “Hear me! Hear me! Hear me!”

His voice reached me like the cry of a child lost in a storm. He asked what was happening to the earth, for the land labored beneath him and shook so fiercely that he feared he would perish with it. Then a great disturbance passed through creation, a voice sounded from heaven, and Noah fell upon his face.

I came near and stood beside him.

“Why have you called to me with such bitterness?” I asked.

I told him that a command had gone forth concerning the inhabitants of the earth. Humanity had received secrets and turned them toward oppression: hidden arts, sorceries, powers taught by rebellious spirits, knowledge by which the treasures beneath the ground were drawn forth and shaped according to human desire. Men had learned to reach deeply into creation, but they had not learned the wisdom necessary to carry what they found.

This was the wound I had seen before. Knowledge itself shines like a blade beneath the sun, neither righteous nor wicked until it is taken into a hand. But when knowledge joins itself to greed, fear, and the hunger to rule, what might have served life becomes an instrument by which life is wounded.

I took Noah by the hand and raised him from the ground.

“Go,” I told him. “I have asked the Lord of Spirits concerning the trembling of the earth.”

Then I gave him the word I had received. Judgment had come because violence had multiplied beyond measure, but Noah had not been forgotten within it. The Lord knew him, knew the purity of his heart, and would preserve him amid what was coming.

I looked upon my descendant and saw fear still living in his eyes, but now another thing stood beside it: hope.

The earth could shake. Kingdoms could fall. Secrets could be corrupted, angels rebel, and darkness spread until it seemed to cover every horizon. Yet the Lord of Spirits could still take hold of one faithful life and carry through it a seed into a world beyond the ruin.

“Your name will be established among the holy,” I told Noah. “Your seed will be established in righteousness.”

Then I released his hand.

Around us the earth still trembled.

But trembling is not the same as abandonment.
